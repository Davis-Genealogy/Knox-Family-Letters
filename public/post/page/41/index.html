From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed: February 4, 1887 from R.P.O Miss Jeanette Knox Sidney, Ia. Heewaut Co. Pearl Feb. 4th ’87 Dear Nettie Your warm, kind letter of love, just received and read with great pleasure. Will now try and answer too. Has been a long time since I heard from you but I hope to see you soon then that will pay for waiting. Mrs. Attwell died last night, funeral service tomorrow at the church house. They have sent for Brother Authey to lead the funeral and have not got any answer yet. I don’t know whether he will agree or not. I got a letter from Mr. Weedles today. He said if I thought Jae could do the work I might send him to Sinclair next Tuesday morning. Now I am going to let him try it. If he works a failure I can’t help it. Sinclair is a small place and not hardly any thing to do so if he can’t get along there he can’t anywhere else. Yes Jae and Will Maymard are going with the Well’s girls and I think they are both pretty boldly gave. I am all right today. I had the tooth taken out before I received your advice about it. I went to Sailor’s Lament last night at the church house. it was very good. Yes I think you had better write when you will reach Whitehall as a letter might be delayed. The Crusher is not working this week they have a breakdown. There will be a good change for a sleigh ride yet this winter. Although there aint any snow on the ground here but it looks like snowing. Now I have told you all. I love to write to you but hope I won’t have to write any other time. Write please, see you soon. Yours truly, E.H. Chandler ...

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on February 1st ’87 Sidney, Iowa Feb 1st ’87 Mr. E.H. Chandler My Dearest Friend, Your most welcome letter received last eve and perused with the greatest of pleasure and now I will try to write a few lines in answer. It seems if you have the same kind of weather there as we have here, I will get to take a sleigh ride with you sure enough if it does not turn warm and thaw the snow off before I get there. It is snowing very fast this am and there is a good deal of snow already on the ground. There is no fear of my changing my mind about coming home for I am only to anxious to get started. I expect I had better wire you what time I will start. Annie think stat would be best. I am truly sorry to hear of you suffering so much with your tooth. Annie say’s for me to tell you that she said for you to have that tooth taken out immediately. So I guess you had better do so. I have not received my money from home yet as Maggie said she would send it in next letter. I expect it today or tomorrow. Then after that gets here there is no telling what time I will rack out for home but I should say, rather shortly. I have felt sorry many times because I did not answer that letter of yours because since then, instead of getting two letters from you, I only get one in the same length of time and it seems such along time to wait for one letter comes until I get another from you, but Oh! how nice to think, we will soon see each other and then can talk instead of writing. Not that I tire of writing oh no – I have always found that a pleasure. But it will give me more pleasure still, to see and talk to you. I suppose the fox chase was quite an exciting affair for the people of Peal and other towns. But I fear the next report I hear of it will be that the crowd all stayed at Pearl the most of the night and got drunk and had a general tear up. That is what I expect to hear in next report. Yes – if Johnie had sen there he would also have been in the five and I think Nettie would have been too, if she had been there. Does Joe try to go with anybody? When I was at home the girls (Lizzie and Maggie) said he had been going some with May Hess. But I never seen them together while I was there. That is too bad that Matt Mulligan was so unfortunate as to loose his position as night watch by that explosion. I suppose he can not get a job at the grocery store any more at all. Well I am surprised to hear of Sell Strunk’s break, as I was in hopes Sell would change his ways after George’s death and after his joining the church but it seems he has failed. Well now I will close. John has just returned from the office and I did not get a letter from Maggie. Write soon. Yours Lovingly, Nettie ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on January 19, 1887 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co Pearl, Ill Jan 19, 1887 Dear Nettie, I just received your kind letter of 16th will try and answer this pm. I was glad to hear from you and to hear you have concluded to come home. Well if you can let me know what day you will start for home. If you come by the way of White Hall let me know what day you will be there and I will meet you in Whitehall and if you should take a notion to come soon other way let me know. I was surprised when Maggie told me the other day that you was coming home. You can imagine about how glad I was but I can’t tell you. When you get this letter and are going to start soon. Answer about 2 days before you leave and be sure and say what day you expect to reach Whitehall. Now will not write but a short letter this time but hope to see you soon then I can tell you all. I am busy today. Mae is sick. I guess he has not been at the depot today. Oh, yes we had quite an explosion at the quarry last night or this morning, the powder house exploded. No one hurt as there was no one there but Matt Mulligan and he happened to be out of the way. There was 7 hundred pound went off. It made a big rocket. It woke me up. So you may know it had to be pretty loud. Now will close hoping to hear from you soon. I am yours truly, E.H. Chandler ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on January 15, 1887 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia. Freemaut Co. Pearl, Ill Jan 15th ’87 Dear Nettie, I received your kind letter yesterday and was glad to hear from you again as I always am. I will try and answer this morning but don’t know how I will make it. Nothing to write about. They are going to have a festival at the church house next Friday night. They had a county meeting last night. Everybody is going to the fox chase today. I was glad to hear you are getting all right again. Yes I suppose the Dr. thought you was a good patient. He gave a visiting when he went to see you. I will be glad when your time is out in Iowa. It seems to me like you have been gone 6 years now. I let Maggie read the letter you wrote in answer to the one I told you about falling down on the platform. She liked to have taking a fit about it. I think the snow will go off and spare the sleighing if it don’t turn cold pretty soon. This morning looks like a spring morning. Well I will be glad when spring comes again. I am getting tired staying in side so much. When does Johny think he will come home? Yes Nettie your letters are all interesting to me whether they are long or short. I don’t think Jae will get a job soon but he could if he was a good operator. Theres a good chance for Operators now on this road. I thought yesterday I would see what Jae would do if he was alone. So I told him when the conductor signed the order to get it corrected. I was out in the passenger room. Jae gave the conductor signature to the order. Then the dispatcher asked him if the train was all ready to go. Jae near made him any answer then asked him again and Jae asked him what he said. Then the dispatcher said where is E. By that time I went in and fixed them up. It seems to me that Jae don’t try to learn anything. I have gave him a good chance but I am not going to recumbent him until I know he can do what they want him to do. Jae has made a move on all of the Wells girls. Him and Billy Maynard both go out to Wells. Now they are awful thick. Now I will close for this time. Hopeful to hear from you soon. With all my love to you. I am yours Truly, E.H. Chandler ...

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on January 12, 1887 Mr. E.H. Chandler Dear Lize, Your most welcome letter received and contents perused with pleasure. I suppose you will not find this letter very interesting and am at a lose to know or think of anything to write at a lll. So if you find it very dry, please excuse for this time and perhaps I can do better next time. I have been doing lots of running around the past two or three days. Last Monday I spent the day with Miss Mary Hicks and yesterday I was working on fancy work all day at the milliner shop and today will go again. So you see I am making up for when I had to stay at home. Monday while I was at Miss Hick’s the Dr. came up to see how I was getting along and low and behold I was visiting. I guess he thought I was a rather pert patient. Don’t you think so too? yes, I guess Willie is pretty tired of Pearl by this time but Pa said in one of his letters to me that he was going to try to get Willie to stay the balance of the winter there at home. But I fear he will not succeed for I think Willie would nearly die to stay there and have nothing to do. That was too bad about that poor man that had his legs cut off and I think it was a good thing that death did claim him as he always would have been such a bad cripple. Do you know what his name is? This is a lovely day and so pleasant. There is a splendid sleighing snow on the ground and the sleigh just more than fly. Where does Scrooge work about? I suppose Joe thinks he is competent to be Opr. Agent and all in one. Will he get a job and if so, where? I think the different R.R. Co’s were very good to remember you so kindly by sending you such presents as you named in you letter. Well I would like to have seen Lizzie and Maggie when their sleigh broke down. I know I would have laughed. Has Pa got a sleigh or who’s do they use. Well I must now close and go up town to my work. Good-bye with my love to you. I am ever yours, Nettie (I would like to talk to you 2 or 3 days_ Write Soon. ...

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on January 9, 1887 Sidney, Iowa, jan. 9th, 18887 Sunday Eve. Mr. E. H. Chandler Dear Lige, Your most welcome letter received yesterday and contents read with pleasure as it had been a long time it seemed to me since I had heard from you. It took my letter of the 1st longer time to reach you than usual. I wrote you another before that one dated December 29th I believe. It surely did not reach you as you said you had been looking for a letter from me for 2 or 3 days. and I did not get any answer to it. I need your telegram today about 1/2 past 3 o’clock. I am sorry you were so uneasy about me but I appreciate your kindness to me more than I can express. I did not answer your telegram as I am sure Maggie received a letter from me today and it will say how I am and knowing they would let you know I thought it no use to answer the telegram as the letter will be there sooner than it anyway. I am now about all right with the exception of a very severe cold. My eyes are now all right except a little weak yet. My face is clear of the breaking – out, but there is some signs of it still on my hands. I have not been, to say, sick at all only while my eyes were so very sore I kept to my bed most of 2 or 3 days. I have not been out of doors this past week but I expect to go up town tomorrow if the day is not stormy. I was so frightened when the Express Agent brought me the telegram today I could scarcely open it. I thought so of you were very sick and perhaps it was calling me home. Oh! how glad I would be to be there. I would give anything to see you but now it is a little over 2 months to wait until that time comes. I am so glad to have your picture when I get to thinking of you I can get your pictures and look at it, if I can’t see you personally. I look at it 5 or 6 times a day. I see you still have that pin with “N” on it. It looms up in the picture. Every time I show it to anyone here they notice the pin and ask me if it is mine. I tell them it used to be. Johnnie and some more of the town boys have been sawing ice. They are not done yet. I tell you we have to get up a little earlier while he is at work. But today being Sunday we did not have to get up so early. While we were at our breakfast, Johnnie said we were eating our dinner and I asked him why he said that. He said it was just twelve o’clock (noon) and I did not believe it and left the table to look at the clock, and sure enough it was 12-5. Wasn’t we early? Do you ever get up so early? Oh! Oh! Mister! You think you would treat me the same as the other girls if I was there. Do you? Well now I guess you wouldn’t, I have something to say about that. I could not help but laugh when I read in your letter about you falling on the platform. It would have been a picnic for Lizzie and Maggie if they had seen you about that time. I am sorry to hear of Willie’s misfortune but I had heard of it before. I suppose he will never find his pocket book not hear anything of it again. Oh! Yes – I suppose Frank Williams has Frank Woods done up. It would not surprise me very much if William and Lizzie marry. Pa said in one of his letters that they was afraid Williams would cause them to offer up another girl before the winter is over. What do you think about it? Say tell Lizzie J. For me that I am just as Mad as I can be at her because she has not answered my letter. I would like to have seen the show you told me about (The Man and Bear) I think I should have enjoyed it ever so much. Oh! I do hope they will succeed in getting the money to build the church with. That would be so nice. I can hardly write as I am using my knee for a writing desk. You will please excuse writing and lead pencil. Now I will close as it is bedtime. Oh! I forgot to tell you that I got my hair combed New Year’s day but I expect if you have been here you could have did a better job of it. I did not style up one but today if it is true. I am getting old fast but I am yours, Nettie ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on December 29, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia. Freemaut Co. Pearl, Dec 29th ’86 Dear Nettie, Your kind letter of 25th received today. I was surely glad to hear from you. Oh yes I got the cup yesterday. Everything in good order I think it is awfully nice and is a very suitable gift. I haven’t used it now yet. Maggie was awful anxious to see what was in the box for her. I opened it then went over to the house to tease Maggie. I told her I got the cup but there was nothing for her. Willie K arrived last night from St. Louis. He thinks he will remain in Pearl awhile. There is king to be a concert Friday night at the church house. Oh yes I got a Christmas card from Miss N. of Jacksonville but I knew it by the handwrite on envelope. There is a good snow on the ground. If you were here I would like to go sleigh riding. I haven’t had a ride this winter. Willie was asking me about Miss Emma. I expect he will be out sleigh riding pretty soon. I think they are going to have a big time at Duke’s Saturday night. I am having a good time this week not much work to do, but I expect to pay for it after the holidays are over. I guess you have a good time buying Christmas presents for the children. We’ll I hope you may have a good time new year, there aint nothing going on in Pearl today and nothing to write about. I believe you can beat me writing good long letters. Well I will have to close for this time. Hoping to hear from you soon. I am your truly, E.H. Chandler P.S. I heard from Phillips today. Jr was talking to him on the well. He is working nights at Edwardsville junction for another road. Lovingly Yours, E. ...

December 29, 1886 · Post Cards

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on December 28, 1886 Sidney, Ia. Dec. 28th ’86 Mr. E.H. Chandler Dear Lige: – You’re most welcome letter received yesterday eve late. I was very glad to hear from you again and to heat of you all begin well as this leaves us all at present. Oh! I know you all had a better time there, Christmas than I did here, I think the tree must have looked awful pretty and I only wish I could have gazed upon it. Oh! I am so tired of staying here and last night after I had retired I was thinking of home and nearly made up my mind that I would write you today to say that I would start for home soon and you could write and tell me where you would meet me, but this morn I thought I would stay a while longer anyway. I would hate to leave Johnnie, and especially as he has not found anything to do, he would be so home-sick if I would go home and Willie gone to. I don’t know when Johnnie does intend to go home. I have not heard him say anything about it only to wish he was there. I think Maggie’s got an awful nice present, also Ma, from the tree. Yours is quite nice also. Who was so thoughtful as to put those on for you? Do you know, I received another present since I wrote you your last. Mr. Hill sent me a large breast pin and a note asking me to accept of it with his best wishes. It is real pretty I think. I hope you and Maggie has received your presents before this and I hope your cup or saucer was not broken when it reached you. Lige please excuse this short and poorly written letter as I will have to hurry to get it in the Riverton mail now and I am going to call at the doctor’s office to have him to do some thing for my face and hands. There is some kind breakout on them and I sure am uneasy about it. Hoping to hear from you soon. ...
