From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Ahrweiler, Germany on March 16, 1918 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill USA Corp. M.W. Chandler Co “C” 117 F.B.S.C. A.E.F. Ahrweiler, Germany March 16th Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Guess it’s about time to write home. Wrote Grace and Ruby few nights ago. Had dandy long letter from Mama yesterday. It was dated 25th of September. Had been to Central post office. Got letter from Papa today dated February 24th. Certainly am tickled to get all your letters. I’ve been getting lots mail lately. General Pershing reviewed the division today. Everything went lovely. He also decorated some of the men with D.S.C.s. Saw in the paper other day where we were to leave here the 1st of April. Said he would go to Antwerp by rail and embark from there. Certainly hope we do leave then and I think we will. The division is turning in their equipment now and all men on leave have been ordered to return to the division by the 24th. We were dropped from the 4th Army Corps today and are now 3rd army reserve so everything points to an early departure. So Lesley Wildt is here on the Rhine eh? Certainly would love to see him. He’s due to stay here for some time if he’s in the Regular Army though. Glad I’m not with the 1st F.B. now. They’re in the 2nd Division stationed not very far from here and guess they’ll be here several months longer. Has been pretty chilly today but it’s generally nice and warm. Golly, can hardly realize that we will be on our way home pretty soon. Guess about all of the Pearl boys are back now except Leo Wildt and myself. Lordy I’ll sure be glad to get back. Guess I’ll get there in time to go fishing with Papa and Knox. Am figuring pretty strong on being in the sates by Easter Sunday. I was in Pexonne, France in the Lorraine Sector. It was a quiet place compared to the rest of the fronts we were on. Guess I wont get a chance to have my picture taken over here but will have some taken soon as I get to the States. Well I must close for this time. Hope to be able to state definitely when we will leave in my next letter. Am feeling great. Hope you are all well. Love to all, Martin Corp. M.W. Chandler Co “C” 117 FBSC A.E.F. ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: France on February 22, 1918 Pvt. M.W. Chandler Co “B” 1st F.B.S.C. A.E.F. Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois USA France Feb. 7th, 1918 Dearest Folks: Thought I’d write a few lines tonight before going to bed. Have heard nothing yet as to when I may be able to join my company. Am anxious to get back o them so can get my mail which I’m sure must be with my Company. Last mail I received was a week before leaving the States. Also like to get my pay and my clothes. We’re having great weather just like spring. Only hope it continues. This certainly is a very pretty country. Everything seems rather ancient. The buildings are all of stone – also the floors. The majority of the French wear wooden shoes. They seem to be very hospitable. I’m picking up a little French language each day. It seems a difficult language to learn. The making and transportation of wine seems to be the greatest industry among the class of people I’ve seen so far. I’m doing nothing now except getting on a fatigue detail occasionally. Rest of time I stay pretty close to my billet in hopes of being called out to be sent my company. Met a Captain in a signal Corps Bat. who was formerly a Lieutenant in our Battalion. He directed me to Division signal officer who took my name and organization and said he would take it up with Chief Signal Officer in an effort to send me back to my organization but said he didn’t know whether or not he could do anything. If I had my service record and description list I could be transferred into a signal corps outfit here but as it is I’ll have to stay with casual outfit. I’ve had quite a bit of training in the states and am first class telegrapher and qualified in all of the visual signaling and I’d like to do what good I can, if any. In the Casual Company we don’t do any drilling and thats why I’d like to get back in my company or else be transferred to another organization. Am getting good meals and feel OK all the time. Only hope you are all feeling as well as I. Intended writing Ruby and Sam before now but don’t know their address. Asked you for it some time ago but of course wont know until I get my mail. Must close and go to bed. Was on a fatigue detail all afternoon and am a little tired. Don’t write anymore until I get what mail you’ve already sent. Love to all, Martin Pvt. Martin W. Chandler Co “B” 1st F.B.S.C. A.E.F Via New York ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Ahrweiler, Germany on January 13, 1918 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill USA Ahrweiler, Germany Jan 13th, 1918 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all, No mail since last writing. Expecting to receive my back mail most any day. Sure be glad when it comes. Nothing new to write about. Still having mild weather. Am working in Message Center here. Recording correspondence, telegrams, etc. Doesn’t require much clerical ability. Work from 8 am to 5 pm and in addition, work half night every fourth night. Don’t have much to do at night but keep pretty busy during the day. have a good warm place to sleep. Getting good meals here. Have been feeling extremely good past month. Notice in paper when they are busting men out of service, at a rapid rate in the states. Will certainly be glad when we get back. Don’t have the least idea when that will be. Hear lots of rumors but, of course, one can’t believe them. The A.E.F. has got an Old Maids sewing circle backed clear of the map when it comes to starting rumors. Was much surprised to hear of Colonel Roosevelt’s death. The Country will sure miss him. Our signal Corps Radio picks up the states, Russia, Honolulu and many other places. Get lots of press from the states. The German people treat us very good. Do almost anything for a fellow. Guess they’re depending a great deal on the U.S. helping them out in the future. Well must close for this time. Hope you are all well. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

January 13, 1918 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Ahrweiler, Germany on January 5, 1918 Ahrweiler, Germany January 5 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received the box of candy and cake yesterday in good condition. Lordy it certainly was good. Twas in good condition. Nothing tasted anything better. Sure glad to get it. Was afraid it had arrived and gone to central post office with rest of my mail before I got back to my organization. I’m staying with a German family now. Have a pretty fair room. Nice and warm. The people treat me just fine. Do almost anything for a fellow. It is rumored we will leave here about the 10th or 12th but dont have the remotest idea where we will go. Hope we go home. Gee I’d certainly love to see you all. I’m extremely anxious to see my new relations. What does Uncle Knox think of Gene and Audrey? He’s a big one to be an uncle twice. Ha. How’s he getting along in school? Expect he has grown considerably since I last saw him. I’ve gained about 15 pounds since the war ended. I was sure glad it ended too. Don’t ever want to go through another war. Hope there never is another one. I’ve had all I want of war. The only part of the big battles I missed was the last part of the Argonne battle. Wish I could have been on the line when it ended. There is one German here where I stay that was in Chateau Thierry and St. Michiel Battles. Seems funny to be friendly with one who, only two months ago, was opposite us in the lines. Soap is very scarce over here. The Germans can hardly get soap at any price. We have soap issued to us and we can also buy it at Commissary at reasonable price. Rubber is also very scarce over here. Leather is used largely as a substitute for rubber. All the German gas masks are made of leather. Still having mild weather. This Rhineland is a very good territory. Great place for tourists. Land is very fertile. Some very pretty towns in this vicinity. Will mail you some postcards of this vicinity in few days. Expect to be paid off in day or two. I have about $35.00 coming. 3 months pay. Hope we start for home when we leave here. Lord if I ever get home! Year ago this time we were out on deck looking for submarines. Ha! It wont take near so long to make the trip back across the Atlantic as coming over. We were 14 days on water coming over. I’ll never cross it again if I ever get back to the U.S.A. Well I must close for this time. Hope to get my back mail in a few days. Will write again soon. Hope everybody is well. Lovingly, Martin ...

December 26, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: New Jersey on December 17, 1917 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike County Dec 16th, ’17 Dearest Folks, Have received 2 letters from Papa since last writing. Guess you’ll think I’ve quit writing altogether. I’ve written twice. But as usual didn’t mail them. They weren’t very long letters anyway. Just a note. Have been working like the dickens past week. It has snowed every day. Now 8:30 pm and we just got through loading our combat wagons, trucks, motorcycles, and other boxes and will load the horses tomorrow and leave Tuesday for sure so you needn’t write me anymore until you hear from me again. Needn’t mention to anyone that we have gone. Will write you as often as possible from over there but don’t expect any descriptions of country, etc cause it isn’t allowed. Got letter from Chas Morgan at St. Louis other day. It was in the dead letter office for some time account he didn’t put my organization on envelope. Will enclose it. Today is Knox’s birthday. Wish I had something to send. I’m broke cause didn’t draw any pay this month. I haven’t read the insurance policy yet. Guess they will mail it direct to you. I changed my mind and made it for $10,000.00 in case I did you will get about $58.00 per month for 20 years and in case of death of beneficiary it goes to next closest relative and sundown until it is all paid. Again if I should be disabled I would receive a pretty nice income. It goes $6.50 per month on $10,000.00. That will leave me about 3.00 per month which I think will be all I’ll need. Did you ever hear in regard to increase on my allotment? Sure hope you get it. I’m feeling fine and have plenty of warm clothing and will make it OK so don’t worry about me. We have a dandy Captain. He is always looking out for our health and comfort. Well I’ll stop and turn in cause I’m sure tired. Here’s wishing everybody a merry merry Christmas and Happy New Years and hope I’ll be with you on the next Xmas. Knox here’s best wishes for a happy birthday and hope I’ll see a big strapping fellow when I get back. Will let you hear from me soon as can. Lots love to all, Martin ...

December 17, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Long Branch, New Jersey on December 7, 1917 Mrs. E. H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois Pike County Dec 6th 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received Mama’s letter today and was nightly tickled to get it. Mama always writes long news letters. Just wrote night before last so don’t know much to write. I will fill out appreciation blank for Insurance tomorrow for $5000.00. It will cost $3.30 per month. I don’t like the way they pay it but can’t help it. It’s this way. If I get killed you will get the $5000.00 at so much per month for 20 years but in Papa’s case I think they will make the payments larger and less time. $5000.00 would be about $25.00 per month for 20 years. It doesn’t pay anything for Partial Disability but pays considerable for Total Disability. They have another Compensation Act which provides for Partial and Total Disability. It’s put up by the government and doesn’t cost us anything. So in case I should be totally disabled I would receive quite a bit per month. Will have them send the policy to you. Mama I sure hope your fruit cake pans out all right cause I’d sure like to have a piece. Still pretty cold here. Don’t know yet when we will leave but think pretty soon. Think they are figuring on motorizing this company. Hope so anyway. We will have Motor Cycles then instead of horses. Well don’t know anymore to write just now so will stop. Everybody keep on writing often. Lots Love, Martin ...

December 7, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed On: December 4th, 1917 Dec 4th 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and All Received 3 letters from Papa, and Mama’s and Knox’s letters closing Grace’s and was certainly pleased to get them all. Sure were dandy letters. Also received the cigars from Papa today. Sure enjoyed them. Got several left. That will save my tobacco a little Intended writing last Friday but kept putting it off. We certainly had a great Thanksgiving dinner. I’ll mail you the menu. It’s too large to enclose. Hope you all enjoyed Thanksgiving as well as I did as far as the dinner was concerned. I’d rather have been home though. Sure was tickled to get Knox’s letter. He does’t seem to think much of Papa’s hunting abilities. Said Papa had gone hunting and got nothing as usual. Ha. Lordy I’d like to be there and go hunting. Say I haven’t got a pair of skates around there have I? The river is Frozen and I’d like to go skating. Can’t buy any this pay day account not getting any pay until January. Didn’t get to sign the pay role for some reason or other. Never mind about them though if there isn’t a pair there. I’ll manage to borrow some skates from some one. I received the Pike County Republic other day. Get it regularly. Noticed my name in it as one of the boys from Pike in service. Wonder who is sending me that paper? Got a dandy letter from Les Wildt other day. Have been corresponding with him ever since you gave me his address. I get a sweater, 2 pair socks and a helmet from the Red Cross today. Sure are nice but the sweater isn’t as good as the one I got from home. I can use them both cause its awful cold out riding each morning. My hands and feet nearly freeze when out riding. I’m going to mail my summer clothes home. Can’t take them to France. I’ve 3 or 4 pair Khaki trousers that would make Knox good hunting clothes to wear over others. Also 4 or 5 suits summer underwear. Haven’t room to take care of them. If I’m still in the army next summer I’ll get more. Got a baseball sweater that Knox might use too. Do you remember me telling you some time ago about getting a card from Alabama N.Y. and no signature on it? I just ran across the card today and will enclose it. Perhaps you can figure who sent it. Looks like Tom Blair’s writing. Was he in Albany month or so ago? We had moving pictures here (YMCA) last night of our company out on wire drill. They were good and plain. I wasn’t in it. Was in another section 5 miles out when the pictures were being taken. Glad the girls got their passes ok. Will write them at Morrisonville. Golly I’d give anything to be home Christmas but no chance. Aside from being unable to get a furlough it would take about $50.00 to make the trip. Besides I don’t know whether or not we’ll be here Christmas. I saw a stencil in orderly room other day marked “Co B 17BSC” port of Embarkation Hoboken, NY but can’t tell a great deal about that. Think they just have the stamp on hand ready in case we do go right away. Well they are putting the lights out to run some movies so must stop. Intended to write more tonight but no light. Everybody write often. Lots Love, Martin ...

December 4, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Little Silver, New Jersey on November 16, 1917 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike County Little Silver, NJ Nov 16th Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received 2 letters from Papa since last writing and mighty glad to get them. Papa sure does write regularly. I’m feeling better today although still have a bad cold. Received the sweater from Bess today. Gee – It’s a dandy letter. Just what I wanted. Came in mighty handy today. T’was awful cold and we were out on a full pack inspection. Yes Papa we have telegraphing each pm on buzzer. We don’t use anything but continental code. Guess I’ll be pretty rusty on Morse when I come out. I’ll sure be glad when this war is over. I’m getting kinda homesick. Lordy but I’d sure love to spend Christmas at Pearl. I don’t practice visual signaling any more nearly all of the company are qualified so don’t have to practice. We have wire drill nearly every day. Also giving as instructions in ciphering. We all have a cipher disc. I don’t think we will go over before spring but of course can’t tell. Was on guard Thursday night. Golly it was cold. Do guard duty about every 12 days. Doesn’t take long for guard to roll around. Time sure is passing quick for me. Can’t realize that I’ve been in the army 6 months. 2 of the sergeants in our company received commission as first Lieutenants today. Don’t know where they will go. Wish they were going to stay with out company. I like them both awfully well. We have all of our winter clothing now. Good warm clothing. Hows Lucerne coming on the piano? Was over to YMCA last night. Had Vaudeville and lunch afterwards. Some people by name of Ruinson pay for all this. Have an entertainment and something to eat every Thursday night. Sure is nice of them. All the people through this country are very hospitable. Well can’t think of much to write tonight so will close. Glad to hear you re all well. I’m feeling good expect for cold. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Margaret Chandler

From: Martin Chandler, To: Margaret Chandler Mailed From: Little Silver, New Jersey on November 2, 1917 Miss Margaret Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike County Nov 2nd, 1917 Dearest Margaret, Received your letter some time ago and sure glad to get it. Wish you’d write often. Hows school coming? Guess you’re having a great time playing basket ball. Gee it will soon be Christmas. Seems an age since I’ve spent an Xmas at home. Sure hope I’ll be at home some Christmas. Don’t know where I’ll be this Christmas. Kinda looks as thought we were going to leave before soon. But can’t tell much about it. Didn’t expect to be here now. Sure is cold here. Having lots rain too. Makes it awful disagreeable. I’m sure having a peach of a time in the kitchen. Lots of work to do. Get up at 5:30 am and work until 6:30 pm. Wash dishes, pans, peel spuds, onions, and do a little bit of everything. Carry coal and cut wood each day. I’ll be off tomorrow night and I’ll sure be glad of it. Wrote Bess last night and put a 2 cent stamp on it so guess she will have to pay a penny to get it out. Didn’t think about having to put 3 cent on it. When are you going to Morrisonville? How is Audrey? Golly Id sure love to see her and the rest of you all. Well I don’t know anything to write tonight so will stop. Be sure and write as often as you can cause I sure love to get the letters. Hope everybody is well. Lovingly, Martin ...
