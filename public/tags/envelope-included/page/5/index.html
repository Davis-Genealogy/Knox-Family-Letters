From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Hillsboro, Texas on January 14, 1916 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike Co Hillsboro, Tex Jan 14, 1916 Dearest Folks, Thought I’d write few lines this am. I’ve been going on so much about how warm it was and night before last it dropped to 10 degrees about zero. I was in a box car all night and it was certainly cold. I didn’t go to Greenville with Mr. Coster as expected. Stayed in San Antonio another day trying to get a job. I came to Hillsboro and have been treated fine. The above company have their bunk cars here and they have me my meals and the foreman gave me a splendid letter to W.L. Hilt to help me towards getting a job. Says it don’t pay very much to start but good chance for promotion for right man. Sure have reacted me like a prince. I’ll go from here to Dallas and then to Greenville to get my clothes and then to Ft. Worth to see this company. Ft. Worth is close to Dallas but will have to go to Greenville to get my clothes account can’t have them forwarded. With the letter I have, I certainly ought to be able to get something. Will send you a copy of the letter. It is about 20 degrees above zero this am and am sure glad I’ve a good warm place to stay while I’m here. This Mr. Allen who gave me the letter hadn’t know me but about 5 hours when he gave it to me. Sure is a nice man. Well I’ll write you soon as get to Ft. Worth and see what I can get. Hope everything is well and OK. Lovingly, Mart ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailing Address: Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois Pike County Return Address: M. Chandler COB 1st BSC Mammouth Parking 9 am Indianapolis, Mo. 18th Dearest Papa, Mama, & all En route to Mammouth Park, Mo. Cmae through K.C. & St. Louis both but was only in each place 15 minutes and was way down in yards. We weren’t allowed to leave train at all. Just now pulling into Maple. Intended to write you from KC but no chance. We came through St. Louis at 11 pm last night. Rock bends El Paso to St. Louis and then St. Louis to destination. Should reach New Jersey about night. All the trains are taking siding for us and we have had a clear track since leaving Mt. Bliss. Delayed occasional by broken tunnel on one of the car horses and then we have to unload them every 3 hours and let them rest 8 hours. All the little towns in Missouri and Kansas had word of our coming and school was dismissed and they were all down to station with flags to wave at use. One girl passed out a basket of cigarettes. from way they are rushing us am inclined to think we will go on over to France although some say we are going to camp in NJ for winter. We have dandy pullman and good meals. Having fine trip. Didn’t come through Wichita. Wanted to bad too. Came through Hutchinson, Kans. Well I’m in hurry so can mail this here in Maples cause don’t know when we will stop again. Will write you just as soon as get to New Jersey and give you my address. Love to all Martin (over) P.S. Suppose you get the Battalion picture my picture isn’t a bit good. Can hardly be recognized. Am in the sixth grow from the right and third row right next to the big black horse on cart. ...

From: Vinnie, To: Margaret Knox

From: Vinnie, To: Margaret Knox Mailed From: Milton, Ill. on February 14, 1907 Miss Margaret Knox Pearl, Ill. Grover is here had to resend letter. He has his money. Milton, Ill. Feb 13, ’07 Dearest Margaret, Here is the three dollars – all of Grover’s currency that I know anything about. Wish I could see you to have a long talk. Find out, if you can, where he is going. I don’t know what he means by the way he is acting. He don’t seem to know himself. I am busy today making wigs for the play. I think it will be real good, Bro Campbell stopped in this a.m. and volunteered to begin church earlier so we could begin out play at 8. You know we are having a big meeting. Sixteen were baptized Mon. night. Our new piano is just grand. Some of the “cranks” call it “Mrs. Allen and Johnston’s piano.” We don’t care for we want them all to know how hard we worked for it. We have special music every night and really I never heard such sermons as Rev. Campbell preaches. They say he is the best preacher that was ever in our pulpit. I love him as a man too – He is so good. We are all getting along fine and are happy. Oh I could tell you so much, if I could see you. I’m happiest really than I’ve been for three years. I have certainly had a steady job tending the phones for a long time and at home class, but I’m only glad to do it, as Doc seems so much better satisfied when he knows I’m at the phone to answer the calls. Asa is doing fine. He has been so good to me and is really doing fine. Well Dearie it is time to go practice so I’ll have to quit. If you answer of course, you know enough not to mention about me referring to our happiness, but I know we all are come up when you can. Write and tell me all about Jess and everybody. Tell Aunt Nettie I’ll write to her soon and that I am not angry. No – why should I be – I write about as often as she does after all – thats not at all. My arm is so sore I can hardly write at all. From your friend, Vinnie ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Santa Margarita, California M.W. Chandler Santa Margarita, Cal 8034 Santa Margarita Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike Co Dearest Papa, Mama, and All Received letter from Papa yesterday and sure glad to get it. I’ve been working 2nd trick for last 2 days. 2nd trick man laid off and man they sent to relieve him couldn’t handle 2nd account so much clerical work to do. Don’t think he can handle 3rd either. Agent wired them that he was putting me on 2nd. Gee I have lots of clerical work to do. Have at least 10 car loads of wood, ore, and apples, each day. Ore all goes to Niagara Falls. Costs $750.00 per car to ship it. Have both Ford and Reed abstracts to make out, damage reports, write up freight bulls in cash book, have to make out Western Union and Tuckit report at end of month. Work from 4 pm to 12 but generally come to work at noon. Came over at noon today and made out ticket report and Western Union up to date. So wouldn’t have so much to do tomorrow. I’ll be on this job for about a week longer and when regular 2nd tick man comes back agent said he would see that I got the 3rd trick back. He came over to hotel and awakened me and asked if I would work 2nd trick. I like the job OK. Get lots of good sleep now. I have 3 passenger trains to sell tickets for and both locals and 2 regular freight trains on this trick but don’t have to get outside of office. Local conductors unload freight and wheel it in warehouse and seal cars and baggage boy handle the passenger trains and check baggage. All I have to do is make out switch lists and clear them. Have quite a few orders but not as many on 3rd trick. Both locals get along between 10 and 11 pm. Only thing I don’t like about this job is having to work evenings. I’ve been going up to see a bunch of school teachers every night and have lots of fun. Two of them are Catholic and I get into some awful arguments with them some times. All nice girls though. Always have some candy made or a bunch of fruit. Have a swell Victrola and two of them play the piano. How’s Knox getting along in school? Is Uncle Jim any better? Hope Aunt Sarah is ok. Well must stop and get busy. Hope you all write soon. Lucerne I haven’t heard from you for a long while. Guess Hipockets is waiting till he learns to spell before he writes. Ha. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on January 19, 1887 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co Pearl, Ill Jan 19, 1887 Dear Nettie, I just received your kind letter of 16th will try and answer this pm. I was glad to hear from you and to hear you have concluded to come home. Well if you can let me know what day you will start for home. If you come by the way of White Hall let me know what day you will be there and I will meet you in Whitehall and if you should take a notion to come soon other way let me know. I was surprised when Maggie told me the other day that you was coming home. You can imagine about how glad I was but I can’t tell you. When you get this letter and are going to start soon. Answer about 2 days before you leave and be sure and say what day you expect to reach Whitehall. Now will not write but a short letter this time but hope to see you soon then I can tell you all. I am busy today. Mae is sick. I guess he has not been at the depot today. Oh, yes we had quite an explosion at the quarry last night or this morning, the powder house exploded. No one hurt as there was no one there but Matt Mulligan and he happened to be out of the way. There was 7 hundred pound went off. It made a big rocket. It woke me up. So you may know it had to be pretty loud. Now will close hoping to hear from you soon. I am yours truly, E.H. Chandler ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on December 4, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co. Pearl Ill Dec 4th ’86 Dear Nettie, I will try and answer your kids letter that I received yesterday. I was truly glad to hear from you as I am always thinking what day I will get a letter from you but this time I am afraid it will be a good while for I have not answered the other letter I received 3 or 4 days ago. I will ask you to excuse me this time and I’ll promise not to do so anymore. I have been so busy I did not have time to write. The river has froze over and all the freight comes by rail. I had 15 thousand pounds freight to take care of yesterday and had the crew of the Mattie Bell here. They all went to St. Louis. Just fourteen number. There are lots of trains running and I am trying to get out my reports now. So you can see that I have been usually busy. Yes I am sure you will enjoy yourself while Johny is there till I hope he will find a good situation for the winter. Yes I think most any thing will beat railroading. We had an election on the 1st of this month. Sam McKee Flatan and Will Clauinger Flatan. Got 9 Votes. McKee 47 and Clauinger 48. I do not know where George Steelewell lives. His wife is at home but George has skipped out. I suppose you have heard of his trouble have you not. Every body is having a good time in Pearl. Frank Wood is in town I think. Lizzie J is having a good time. There hasn’t been anything new in Pearl since I wrote to you last. If you was here I could tell you lots but I can’t write it. Haven’t been at church for a week. There is church, a green pond tonight if you was here we would just got flying as there is a good snow on the ground. I can not write a very long letter this time but the next time I will not be so busy then I will write you a long letter if can think of enough to write. Winslow and get a long bass. He is awful good to help. Now Nettie I must close. Have a hard days work before me hoping to hear from you soon. I Remain Yours Truly E.H. Chandler ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on November 3, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co Pearl Ill 3rd ’86 Dear Nettie, Your very kind letter just received but not before I was looking for it. I think it has been a week today since I got a letter from you. I will try to write you a few lines today so can send it off on this eve train. I have so awful much to do today I can not write much but will finish tonight. I don’t know how I would pass the time away if was not for the hopes I have in the future. When you are lonesome and thinking of home you can always remember that I think of you every day and want you to come home as bad as you want to come. My work for the month has to be done up now. Yesterday was Election day and the boys wanted the return by wire and I agreed to get them from 35 states for six dollars. So I have not had time to do anything today only copy bulletins of the election. Oh yes I will be pleased when I get the hat works. I must close now but will write you a letter tonight and give you all the news. Then you will receive it the next day after you get this. Give Anna my regards. I remain yours. Truly, EHC ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on September 25, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co. Pearl, Ills 9/25 Dearest Nettie, Your kind and welcome letter received today. You don’t know how glad I was to hear from you nor can I tell you however it found all wall. Nettie I haven’t the least objection to you keeping company with Mr. Hill. I want you to see all the pleasure you can. I am perfectly satisfied when I have the assurance that you will remain true to your promise. I do not expect to keep company with any one while you are away altho I could keep other company for ever and know that my love for you could not be changed. My affections are to prematurely fixed ever to be changed. Your absence is making me a many a lonesome hour. I hope you will make up your mind to come home soon. I go out on the porch same times of evenings but don’t stay long. I can’t keep from thinking about you. I have opened my trunk every night to look at the picture of one that I have learned to love. I received your telegram OK at 9:45 pm. Same date but I had gone to bed. I came to the office at 8:30 to see if any had came. I was glad to hear your answer. OK I listened at My 13 Instrument all day expecting to hear some one call me. The first thing I done in the morning was to call Bloomington and Kansas City up and ask them if they had anything for me. I was expecting to go to Louisiana or White Hall today but it is raining so hard I will have to give up the notion. If possible I will go one day next week. If I do I am going to get you the ring I promised you. Then I will ask you to wear it in remembrance of the promise. I will go to church tonight. The protracted meeting closed on Tuesday night. There was 71 joined all together, nothing new in Pearl. Your mother went visiting yesterday up to Mr. Betts. Maggie told me yesterday she got a letter from you also as to your regards to my self – many thanks. I am all alone today. Jae has gone up to see his sister. I wrote the Opr a letter at Sidney and told him to destroy the message. Then we wont have to make any report to the company about it will save paying the charges. I saw Lizzie Frogen the other eve. She said Lau want to see Miss Button every evening. Bro Anthony stayed all night at our house Tuesday night. Went off on morning train. I got a letter from Soruby yesterday. He is working at railroad for a few days. Bill Stewart is going to leave Pearl this week. I am going to have a lot of passengers from Pittsfield Monday morning for St. Louis exposition that will be a feather in my cap for them to leave the Wabash Road and come to Pearl. Well I must close lest I should make you tired. Hope this will reach you safely and find you well. Regards to all and love to all. Yours Truly, E.H. Chandler P.S. Write Soon ...
