From: J. Saul and Elijah H. Chandler, To: Unknown Chandler

From: J. Saul and Elijah H. Chandler, To: Unknown Chandler Mailed On: November 17, 1885 Nov. the 17th 1885 Dear Brother, I will write you a few lines past to let you no that I am well and hope this will fin you the same. I haven’t any news to write. Times is dull hear at present. The weather is cold and damp. I have been working in the Creek for the last 2 weeks. Done well part of the time. Our rail road has not been built yet. Don’t think it will be completed this winter. Our working men are all back in Colorado at present. I don’t know whether they will com back this winter or not. I hope not as the winter time is not a good time to look at armies. I had a letter from one of them the last mail. He said if they come it would be soon so I don’t no whether they will come or not. There haven’t been no girls hear but in town there are some. You bet one to cook that would be way up here. I guess not but my lady neighbor that J. spoke of once before and I have had a spell. Well as there is no news I will quit. Write soon and after tell john and Sasha I would like to see them. Goodbye J. Saul Chandler E. H. Chandler ...

From: Linnie Samuels, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Linnie Samuels, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Riverton, Iowa on August 31, 1885 Miss Nettie Kox Pearl, Illinois Riverton, Iowa Aug 31st 1885 Miss Nettie Knox My Dear Friend, I received your welcome letter some days ago and I have been over to Sidney the past few days and so I have not had any time to write since I came home. No. – Mr. Talbot does not intent to return. Mr. Ellis left last Tuesday, a week from today, I just came up from Hamburg as he got on. He said to give you and your brother his kindest regards the next time I wrote. Mrs. Jackson did not give his party as she expected for she had no help. She was very sorry I saw he Sunday and she and Uncle sends their regards and all the Riverton boys and girls you are acquainted with. I suppose you were out to the “Poor Farm” when you were here! I was there last Saturday My brother John that lived with us has moved to Riverton, he moved yesterday. I saw Miss Ellie Cromwell last Thursday and gave her your regards and she sends many in return. I am going up to Riverton this afternoon so I must close. Please give my regards to all. From Your Friend, Linnie Samuels ...

From: William Thomas Knox, To: Anna Roush

From: William Thomas Knox, To: Anna Roush Mailed From: Riverton, Iowa on April 10, 1885 Mrs. Anna Roush Sidney, Iowa Riverton, Iowa, Apr. 10, 1885 Anna Roush, Sidney, Ia. Dear Sister, Enclosed find $15.00 all the change we have in the store this evening. IF you need more let me know and I will furnish it as I don’t want you to be out anything. I wanted to give Nettie some money while here and told her if she needed anything to let me know it. I want her to look as nice and neat as any of the girls and she shall if I can do anything to help it. I seen Delia Sunday eve. and she said she would call on Nettie soon. I received an invitation this eve. from Sidney to attend a party of Laird’s on Friday evening and will decline not knowing whether Nettie will be ready to go. They telephoned to me to know whether I would come and will answer in the morning. Hope Nettie will be ready to go out soon. Your Bro, Will ...

From: Kate G., To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Kate G., To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Milton, Illinois on October 27, 1884 Milton, Ill. 10/28/84 Miss Nettie Knox Pearl Dear Friend, I received your unexpected, welcome epistle this last week and I was so glad to hear from you. I had begun to think you had forgotten me entirely as I never received yours before the 4th of July. I have been going to school ever since Sept. and have no done much visiting not went any thing. We have a dandy Professor this term. His name is Hand. They have placed those looks back and made two rooms up stairs. Mr. Farris has one room. Do you go to school if not come up and see me or if you do come Friday with the mail Saturday if you can not miss school and H.G. and I will take you back Sunday. How does that strike you by this; you see H.G. and I still cling together. Dick Mc told you a he ’cause Stan and Link has not busted. Frank Foreman of Detroit goes with Lill Watts. Bert Hanks of Detroit goes with Ada. Otis Smitherman went with Mary S for a while but they are broke now. Nina Lester went with Tom Morris but bused I believe now. Mary S never told me none of adventures while in Roadhouse but she told the other girls and she don’t talk about anything now only that she has no more sense than when you hired her. I suppose you found that out did she stop at your house. I thought something of coming down after the 4th of July but never got an answer to my letter and supposed you didn’t want me very bad. Nellie Davis will read here Wednesday eve. DOn’t know whether I will go or not. Milton is awful dry nothing to go to only S.S. and church. I heard a little while ago that Lori B was in Milton but she has not went down home, I guess she wont. She has been done since the 6th of Aug. she went up to Astoria where Rox lives and stayed three (3) weeks and came back to Pittsfield and has been working in the hotel as a dining room girl. Poor fool do all of that for the sake of a brainless lad. Well I guess I will close for this rainy Sunday morn hoping to hear from you soon if not sooner. Good bye Yours, Kate G. ...

From: Katie Cumoy, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Katie Cumoy, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Mesquite, Texas on August 10, 1884 Miss Nettie Knox Pearl, Ill Pike Co Mesquite, Texas Aug 10th 1884 Miss Nettie Knox Pearl, Ills. Dear Friend, I received your most welcome letter some time ago. Hope you will excuse my long delay about answering and I will be more punctual in the future. Ma has been in bed sick for over a week it seems as though she will never be well anymore. Will is down with typhoid malarial fever. He has been down over two weeks and is able to sit up in bed now. Since I received your letter I have been having a good time going to meeting there. Have been having meeting two weeks. The Babtise and Presbyterians both having meeting, at once and the churches are not more than two hundred yards apart. They shouted so loud I believe they could have been heard a mile. The first night I was scared half to death for fear that they would get to beating one in the back. I have so much work now that I can scarcely get time to write any more. We are putting up hay and I am going to singing and taking music lessons and it keeps me stirring to get my work all done. I have just finished one letter before yours, and I have two more to write this evening. Tell Ella that I will be sure and write to her before long. Do you ever see Carrie Loandess. I have not heard from her in a long time. So many that have seen your picture have complimented it highly. Tell Ella that I would be pleased to get any kind of a picture of her. Sam sent his best wishes to you and the family and says he would love to see you all. He said that he sent your Pa some papers and want to know if he ever received them. The have brought my horse up and are wanting me to go to the peach orchard for some peaches so I will close with much love to yourself and family. Write soon. Your True Friend, Katie Cumoy ...

From: Katie G., To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Katie G., To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Dallas, Texas on June 1, 1884 Miss Nettie Knox Pearl Station Pike Co Ills. Mesquite, Dallas, Tex June 1st, 1884 Miss Nettie Knox Dear Friend, I thought I would write you a few lines for I have not forgotten you but I expect that you have forgot me ere this. I have waited for you to write for so long, we are all well as common except Pa he is suffering with the tenalgia. Do you have much enjoyment? Now a days I have a very good time considering I live in the country. We live three miles from Mesquite but I have a good riding horse and I get on him of a Sunday and it does not take me long to go to church and Sunday school. There are a great many young folks here and I like them all so well, we have a splendid Sunday school. The average attendance is 100. We are going to have a picnic on the 28th of this month I anticipate on having a splendid time. We have an organ in the church. Manda preforms for them, Eustace and Manda live in Mesquite now. Eustace has a lumber yard there. There is a singing teacher coming out from Dallas to teach a class. There are 42 in the class. I am going to attend. His wife teaches music I expect to take music lessons also. I wish you were here to go with me. Give my love to Ella and tell her to write to me. Please tell me her post office address so I can write to her whether she will write me or not. Give my love to Maggie, tell her I would love to see her, I often think of our good old school days and the romps we used to have to get her. Would love to call them back for awhile were it possible. Please excuse my poorly written letter for I am writing at night while siting up with Pa. With love to the family and a better portion to yourself. I remain a true friend. Katie G. ...
