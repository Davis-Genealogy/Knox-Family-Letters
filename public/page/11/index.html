From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed On: December 4th, 1917 Dec 4th 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and All Received 3 letters from Papa, and Mama’s and Knox’s letters closing Grace’s and was certainly pleased to get them all. Sure were dandy letters. Also received the cigars from Papa today. Sure enjoyed them. Got several left. That will save my tobacco a little Intended writing last Friday but kept putting it off. We certainly had a great Thanksgiving dinner. I’ll mail you the menu. It’s too large to enclose. Hope you all enjoyed Thanksgiving as well as I did as far as the dinner was concerned. I’d rather have been home though. Sure was tickled to get Knox’s letter. He does’t seem to think much of Papa’s hunting abilities. Said Papa had gone hunting and got nothing as usual. Ha. Lordy I’d like to be there and go hunting. Say I haven’t got a pair of skates around there have I? The river is Frozen and I’d like to go skating. Can’t buy any this pay day account not getting any pay until January. Didn’t get to sign the pay role for some reason or other. Never mind about them though if there isn’t a pair there. I’ll manage to borrow some skates from some one. I received the Pike County Republic other day. Get it regularly. Noticed my name in it as one of the boys from Pike in service. Wonder who is sending me that paper? Got a dandy letter from Les Wildt other day. Have been corresponding with him ever since you gave me his address. I get a sweater, 2 pair socks and a helmet from the Red Cross today. Sure are nice but the sweater isn’t as good as the one I got from home. I can use them both cause its awful cold out riding each morning. My hands and feet nearly freeze when out riding. I’m going to mail my summer clothes home. Can’t take them to France. I’ve 3 or 4 pair Khaki trousers that would make Knox good hunting clothes to wear over others. Also 4 or 5 suits summer underwear. Haven’t room to take care of them. If I’m still in the army next summer I’ll get more. Got a baseball sweater that Knox might use too. Do you remember me telling you some time ago about getting a card from Alabama N.Y. and no signature on it? I just ran across the card today and will enclose it. Perhaps you can figure who sent it. Looks like Tom Blair’s writing. Was he in Albany month or so ago? We had moving pictures here (YMCA) last night of our company out on wire drill. They were good and plain. I wasn’t in it. Was in another section 5 miles out when the pictures were being taken. Glad the girls got their passes ok. Will write them at Morrisonville. Golly I’d give anything to be home Christmas but no chance. Aside from being unable to get a furlough it would take about $50.00 to make the trip. Besides I don’t know whether or not we’ll be here Christmas. I saw a stencil in orderly room other day marked “Co B 17BSC” port of Embarkation Hoboken, NY but can’t tell a great deal about that. Think they just have the stamp on hand ready in case we do go right away. Well they are putting the lights out to run some movies so must stop. Intended to write more tonight but no light. Everybody write often. Lots Love, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Little Silver, New Jersey on November 16, 1917 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike County Little Silver, NJ Nov 16th Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received 2 letters from Papa since last writing and mighty glad to get them. Papa sure does write regularly. I’m feeling better today although still have a bad cold. Received the sweater from Bess today. Gee – It’s a dandy letter. Just what I wanted. Came in mighty handy today. T’was awful cold and we were out on a full pack inspection. Yes Papa we have telegraphing each pm on buzzer. We don’t use anything but continental code. Guess I’ll be pretty rusty on Morse when I come out. I’ll sure be glad when this war is over. I’m getting kinda homesick. Lordy but I’d sure love to spend Christmas at Pearl. I don’t practice visual signaling any more nearly all of the company are qualified so don’t have to practice. We have wire drill nearly every day. Also giving as instructions in ciphering. We all have a cipher disc. I don’t think we will go over before spring but of course can’t tell. Was on guard Thursday night. Golly it was cold. Do guard duty about every 12 days. Doesn’t take long for guard to roll around. Time sure is passing quick for me. Can’t realize that I’ve been in the army 6 months. 2 of the sergeants in our company received commission as first Lieutenants today. Don’t know where they will go. Wish they were going to stay with out company. I like them both awfully well. We have all of our winter clothing now. Good warm clothing. Hows Lucerne coming on the piano? Was over to YMCA last night. Had Vaudeville and lunch afterwards. Some people by name of Ruinson pay for all this. Have an entertainment and something to eat every Thursday night. Sure is nice of them. All the people through this country are very hospitable. Well can’t think of much to write tonight so will close. Glad to hear you re all well. I’m feeling good expect for cold. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

Glad to know Hulsingler wasn’t looking for me but was sure he didn’t have anything on me. I’ll probably be here for two weeks or so longer. Getting along OK. It’s getting pretty cool out here. Ralph is getting quite a stay at Rush Hill isn’t he? Wonder why they don’t put Micky to work. Guess I’ll write Bess a letter tonight but I wrote her last. Business isn’t very heavy here now. Never have over 4 or 5 trains during the night. Had a derailment close to here other night and 3 passenger trains had to stay here all night until they got the track cleared. Well must stop now. Will write more often after this. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Chandler Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Chandler Family Stationary Info: Signal Corps Recreation House (National Leauge for Women’s Service) P.O. Little Silver N.J. Tel. 291 Red Bank October 20th, 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all, Received Papa’s letter yesterday and sure glad to get it. Been on guard last 24 hours. Rained all night and most of the morning but, of course, that doesn’t stop one from walking post. I didn’t have my raincoat with me and I was soaked clear through. Awful cold too. The Radio Detachment left today for France. Again, some rumors that we will leave shortly but you can’t feel a thing about it. May go at any time and again may not go at all. We wear two identification tags now. Each one has our name, rank and organization on it. One to be buried with us in case of death and the other one sent to parents. So if you ever get a little round tag with my name on it you can hang out the black ribbon. I’m not worried about not coming back. Think will make it OK in case we should be sent over. Suppose you read of the American Trouspers being torpedoed by German submarine. Fortunately it was returning and not many aboard to lose though and that’s bad enough. We are having a field meet in this camp next Wednesday. All sorts of contests. I haven’t entered in any of them. Don’t think I could compete very strong in any of the athletic events. We’re having a wire contest though, between different Battalions to see who can set up radio station and open telegraph stations in shortest length of time. Of course I’ll take part in that. Awful glad Knox’s arm is mending rapidly. Hate to see him miss so much school. A fellow in our company received a letter from one of the boys in France today. Was dated “France, September 15th” and had been censored. Didn’t say a thing about their locations or of the conditions existing over there. Was over a month on the road so If I get over there and you don’t hear from me very often, you’ll know the reason why. Yes it’s only four more days until papa’s birthday. Wish I had something to send him. Sure hope the men on Ca get a raise. Prices keep going up but not so the wages. Lordy I’d like to be back at work. Sure work in the Army. Fatigue nearly all the time when we’re not drilling and drilling isn’t any play. First thing after Breakfast we police up the camp. Pick up all the matches, cigarette butts, pieces of paper, etc. First call for Reville (Rev-a-le) at 5:45 am. Assembly 6 am, water and feed horses immediately after reville. Breakfast at 6:30 am then clean up camp, as I mentioned above. After get camp cleaned up we groom the horses up to 7:45 am, Calisthenics 7:45 to 8:30 am, mounted drill 8:30 am to 11 am, groom horses on return tom drill then water, feed and put on horse blankets. 1 pm to 2 pm we have visual signaling, 2 pm to 3 pm Buzzer practice. From 3 to 4 pm have nothing to do. 4 pm water and feed horses, 5 pm stand retreat and then we’re all through for the day. Friday evenings we clean equipment instead of drilling. Saturday morning is inspection and Saturday evening and all day Sunday have nothing to do except grokm and feed horses. Mounted drill on Monday is tactical, tha is =, opening telegraph stations and stringing wire cross country. The company is divided up into 4 sections, each section consisting of about 14 men, sometimes not that many. Each section taken wire cart and reel out wire across country, each connecting with one of the other sections so as to establish a complete line of communication between all sections. Each section opens two stations. The first station relating all messages to next section and so on until message has reached destination. Each section forms groups of four numbering 1 to 4 from left to right. No 1 is operator, no 2 and 4 Linemen, and No 3 the horse holder. When command is given to open station the first set of four rides around to reel at cart, reel cart being in rear of each section. All dismount dn turn their horses over to Horse Holder (No.3). The operator gets a Buzzer, ground rod, pencil and pad out of reel carts and connects onto wire, thus opening first station. The reel cart then drives on feeling out wire as it goes. The do it in a gallop. The men who didn’t take part in opening first station ride ahead of the feel cart in the formation I mentioned before and the two linemen follow the cart and see that the cart and see the wire is put in proper shape and no breaks. They patrol the line continually between stations bring responsible for its maintenance and if they find it broken at any point or someone has cut it they immediately replace it. Sometimes the Captain will cut it to see if the linemen are on the job. After we get through sending messages ect., command is given to close station. Then ge outs his instrument and other articles back in. Cart and the reel cart winds up on reel. Two men ride behind with pike poles (pool with hook on end so wire will slip through it) and see that it doesn’t get tangled up. Doesn’t take very long to string out 5 or 10 mile of wire. All sections assemble and come back into camp. On Tuesdays and Thursday we have equilation. Think I mentioned that before. Riding without feet in stirrups, etc. Anyway I dread it most of all. It’s purpose is to train us to balnce ourselves in saddle. Wednesday and Fridays we have company drill, close order. Each section is formed in sets of fours same as in other drill except it doesn’t make any difference how we are placed or whether we’re number 1, 2, 3, or 4. This drill is just to teach us different formations such as “right, or left, about,” “right or left front into line,” “right on left flank,” so that Commander on Battle field can get his company into whatever formation he sees fit by giving the command. well it’s getting pretty late so I’ll close and go to camp. Can’t describe very well, just what we do but its not a bit complicated. Hope this finds every body well. I’m feeling fine, physically. Guess the fatigue we get is good for us. Everybody write soon and Happy Birthday to papa, Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Fort Bliss, Texas on August 27, 1917 M.W. Chandler Co “B” 1st F.B.S.C. Ft. Bliss Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois Pike County Co “B” 1st FBSC Ft. Bliss Texas Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received 2 letters from papa since returning from hike last Saturday evening and sure glad to get them. We went up into New Mexico on the hike. Had a dandy time. The company was split up and strung out through the country and we had continuos wire communication which we put up ourselves. I stayed on duty until midnight. A and C company were along too. A company is the Radio outfit. They put up their wireless apparatus and were in communication with El Paso all the time. They also had a wire strung to our camp and out company relayed all messages for El Paso to the Radio Co and they sent them by wireless to El Paso and Ft. Bliss. The man who brought Papa’s letter to me yesterday saw that it was from Pearl and asked me if I knew anyone there. I told him I lived there and he said he was from White Hall. He sleeps in tent next to mine. He is a step brother of Pearl Ellis and said he remembered Ruby and Pearl were at their house one time. His name is Fitzjarrellz. He is Corporal in the company and a nice fellow. He also knows Uncle Will’s family at Manchester and used to go with Lucille. Sure surprised me to meet him. Said he had been at Pearl lots of times playing ball and coming to boat shows and knows lots of the fellows around Pearl. He’s the only fellow I’ve ran across close from home. Our battalion played baseball with 2nd telegraph battalion yesterday and beat them 7 to 3. Captain was sure pleased. I had m picture taken in ball suit with another fellow. Just a Kodak. Will send you one soon as they are finished. Mama did you ever find the Kodaks in my suit case? Well its just 5 min until drill time and I’ve been writing this in an awful hurry so could get it off this am. Hope everybody is OK. Well write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Fort Bliss, Texas on July 11, 1917 M.W. Chandler Causal Camp Ft. Bliss, Texas Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Casual Camp No. 2 Ft. Bliss, TX 7-11 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all No mail since last writing. Expect to be assigned some time this week. I never have received any mail forwarded from Sanderson yet. Should have a good bunch of it pretty soon. Did you get my cards from Juane & Max? Supposed you got my pictures I sent from Sanderson. I’m still doing messenger duty over at Captain’s office. Don’t have much to do. My you ought to see the soldiers drilling here. They get about 3000 out on parade ground and it sure is pretty to see them all moving. They have 6 or 8 bands playing all the time. There’s about 5 camps here at Ft. Bliss. Wouldn’t be surprised if I was assigned to one of them. Quite a bunch of infantry have been sent out of here lately. Some to Eagle Pass, TX, some to Salt Lake City, Utah and some to Syracuse. Wish they would ship me to Illinois. Lordy wish I had come home before enlisting but they told me Tuesday (Day 2 enlisted) that Saturday was last day could volunteer so I thought I’d better get in. I’ll send my clothes home first time I go to Taylor. Got 2 suit cases full. Sure hot down here. These uniforms are pretty warm too. Generally go to bed about 8 or 9 pm. Have breakfast at 6 am. Then after breakfast everybody helps clean up camp except men detailed for special duty. They have to pick up every match and scrap on ground and then sweep the entire camp. Some of them have to wait on tables and some work in kitchens about 5 each. I’ve worked both of them. Say I worked in kitchen. I peeled 4 bushel potatoes; scrubbed tables and washed pots. I’ve worked every job they’ve got here expect to clean toilets. We have a dandy shower bath here. Each of us received a Toilet kit yesterday free of charge. Consisting of towel, shaving soap, brush, razor, shoe brush, clothes brush, tooth brush and box shoes polish and a bag to keep them in. Also got package needles and some thread. We’re allowed about 40.00 worth clothes when we enlist. My outfit ran about 15.00. Got 3 suits summer underwear, 3 pair socks, 1 shirt and 1 uniform and pair shoes. Oh yes, got a pair overalls and jacket too. Each of us have our own mess kit consisting of fork, knife, spoon (large), and one pan. We take pan in kitchen and they fill it up. Always have radishes, onions, bread, butter, and ice tea, lemonade, or coffee on table. I’ve never got up from table hungry. We wash our own mess kit and bring them in and put them away. Have a free moving picture show over a 8th calvary every night. We’re free to go where we please from 5>45 pm to 11 pm except Sundays and holidays. We’re off all day except men detailed for special duty, as I am. I did quite a letter writing today. Took bunch papers to Camp Stewart 10 miles distant and then made several other trips on horseback and I’m pretty tired and dusty so I’ll quit and go take bath. Expect to get a letter from Papa tomorrow for sure. Sure hope everybody is OK. Love to all, Martin ...
