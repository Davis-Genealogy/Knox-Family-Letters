From: Lucila, To: Amelia J. Wiggins Mailed From: Phoenix, Arizona on April 14, 1936 Mrs. Amelia J. Wiggins #725 Waverly St. R-5 Columbus, Ohio If not called for in 30 Days Return it to Miss Nellie Yorks Gem Dr Phoenix, Arizona Phoenix, Ariz April 12, 1936 Mrs. Amelia J. Wiggins Dear Mother, As I have time I thought I would write you a few lines. Hoping my letter will find well. I am all right. How is it down there in Ohio? You You are all having some awful floods from what I seen in papers and what I been told. So I thought I would write to see if you are still alive. I hope that you dont get in those awful floods. We are beginning to have awful hot weather. I like Arizona – that is the weather here, but in summer it gets awful hot here. I feel it here more than I did any place I lived. I sure love to go back to Calif and go to Hollywood. I know all the movie stars. When is you birthday Mother? O yes have you heard anything about my kids. Well mother I must close for this time, as I have one more letter to write. I remember the good times we used to have together. Then I got to go to town and I mail this on my way to town. I sure love to hear from my kids. Have you every read that letter from my daughter she write to you. Well good mother again I say good bye with love to you till I hear from you again will writte me next time. Your daughter Lucia ...

From: Lucy Clarkin

From: Lucy Clarkin, To: Amelia J. Wiggins Mailed From: Ocean Beach, California on August 16, 1935 2062 Venice St. Ocean Beach, California Mrs. A.J. Wiggins 725 Waverly St. Route 5 Columbus, Ohio Aug. 16, 1935 Dear, dear, friend, I promised you a letter in a few days, but i have been very busy. Three meals a day don’t get themselves. Dishes are stacked and washed once a day. Mike usually washes the. He also goes down to his son’s house and washes dishes for his daughter-in-law. She used to say to me when we were first married, ‘What have you got to do? Just your little bit of housework?” I might have asked her the same question but I just smiled. Mike has trained himself into being quite a satisfactory husband. Don’t think I would ever take the job of training a husband. Both Mike and Mr. Aubury has good mothers who did quite a satisfactory job of training them. Wright Aubury was really a saint and his mother a fine woman. She died six days before I married her son, but she wished me to marry him. I am quite sure that Admiral Reeves is a son of one of his cousins. Wright had two cousins: Wright and Frank Reeves. Who published the Congressional Record about 65 years ago. Wright Reeves had no children and Mr. Aubury lived with him. He was a West Point man. General Grand and his wife visited the Wright Reeves family at their country home in Bladensburg. Wright talked to him in the garden, the General asked, “Where do you come from.” he answered Dutch Flats. Wright said that he thought the Gen. could not be so much because so much gold had been mined at Dutch Flats. Now I don’t know myself whether Dutch Flats was in Calif. or Arizona. Tombstone used to be a thriving place. When Wright was 8 yrs. old his father and a law office there. Only one old Chinaman lives there now. Living on $4.00 a month given him by the county. Of course the Commander-in-Chief of the American Navy may be another family of Reeves but he seems to favor Wright in looks. Wright certainly honored his parents. Aug 14 was my 71st birthday. I had quite a little party. Mike’s 8 year old grand daughter made her first cake for my birthday. Her mother frosted it with caramel frosting and coconut. It tasted something like butterscotch. The cake itself was light as a feather. apples, cake cookies and cocoa were the refreshments. Mike’s 2 grandchildren wanted to stay all night, but we are too old to have them here too much. The other children brought me some little gifts, a nice little printed apron and a handkerchief and an English Language jacket. Mr. Clarkin gave me a $1.00 bill. Do you receive the Household Magazine published by Senator Copper, Topeka, Kansas. He has been mentioned by Nuey Long as a possible candidate for the presidency in 1936. If you are not getting it you should be, as I ordered it sent to you about two weeks ago. I got the news over the radio a little while ago, that the airplanes in which Post and Roger were traveling had crashed and that both were killed. I had a hunch that this trip would prove disastrous and wished that Rogers would not go. The government is expected to help out with old age pensions. The president has signed the bill for Uncle Sam to help out. It is said that the aged will not be obliged to sign away there bit of property under the new plan. I told you about my brother. He was in O.S.U. Hospital 17 days. He was pretty bad for awhile. The hunting season is on again in California. There is usually a number of accidents due to carelessly handled guns. I have thought that Celia might show up in San Diego during the Exposition. If she becomes a public charge she will probably be sent back to Chicago. her children may be as anxious to find her as she is to find them. Poor woman. Her mother was terribly sinned against. Have you ever heard David B. Bush talk over the radio. He is heard here at 9.13 AM. Success talks are his own poetry. He tells stories of the failures and final success of successful men. Mike likes corn bread and I make it once a week. It keeps his bowels in good condition. I am eating home grown bananas. We let them ripen on the plant. The plant dies as the trees or rather plant dies. Mike does not care for them, but the orioles hang nests under the big levels every year. Write soon and often. Your Friend with Love, Lucy Clarkin ...

From: Ruby Chandler Mullholland, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Ruby Chandler Mullholland Mailed From: Wichita, Kansas on December 31, 1930 Mailed Address: Mrs. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois Return Address: Mrs. Sam Mullholland 421 E. Lewis Wichita, Kansas Wichita, Kansas December 31, 1930 Dearest Mama and Martin Received your letter yesterday also one from Bess saying John was led past with such a bad back. Said he suffered terribly the past two night. I want to write her again today. I’ve written Grace so maybe I’ll get such debts settled before the new year. I was reminded of my position Saturday on account of dill times. He told me I had done marvelously well, etc and if I needed references to tell them to come to him. I was there just a month. I’m not going to look for anything else for we’ll be moving soon. The van will go to Fredonia for out things Saturday and Sunday and we’ll be in by the 8th. I hope mostly straightened up by that time. It’s petty far out on N. Laurence 3701. Gene will be transferred to Central International Mediate for the new semester. That school is real close to the golf course. Sam will bring him back and forth. Sam goes to work on an oil lease for the present out 3 miles starting the 2nd. So, he is lucky to get anything as many as are having let out. Grove not Bob have anything in sight either. Those of our fame;y who have good positions are in luck sure enough. Bess Doesn’t write so optimistic either but she doesn’t mind any hardships where her family is connected. She surely is big hearted. Her biggest worry is John’s sickness and I surely hope he improves soon. Took sick the next day after Christmas she said. Mama you didn’t say if you got my box of candy. We mailed it the same time as the letter you received. I hope it wasn’t lost. I was surprised about Lucille having another girl. I wonder how Aunt Belle is now? We had cards from Virginia and Dr. I didn’t send one to Va. as I didn’t get her address and I was too busy to get it. Mother Davis is growing weaker. A.V. [Arthur Vincent] provides a nurse and they have a girl, too. So that is all he can do. He just can’t get there often on account of his work. Mother Muir is fine – makes quilts all the time but it costs them plenty to get them quilted. At least $8 a piece and they drive to Sedgewick to take them to a woman who does it for them Gene is raising the roof with his bugle. He likes to practice on it. His vacation lasts until the 4th. Ours up here is the 8th so that gives us 4 days to get our furniture straightened. I’ll have to cook with electricity as we’ll be out beyond the gas main and water line. But the owner says the improvements will be extended right away. The house is brick and seems to be nice enough. Will tell you more about it after we get in. This house belongs to another concern Sam is interested in and the partner seems very anxious for us to get in and look after things. The advantage in going there is the rental is free and that means a lot at preset. You surely have had some game, haven’t you? I’d like to eat rabbit with you. Gene’s out in the sun – bareheaded just tearing around on his roller skates. It is so pretty out. We had the one snow and the river was full of ice blocks at that time but it has been so mild for a week. I surely hope it will remain mild for the most of the winter and for your sake I hope you don’t have such cold weather if you have to be in Pearl. That house is too hard for you to manage through the winter and I wish I could help you, but it will be rather difficult for me too so I’ll be glad when spring comes. I surely hope Martin is better by now. Tell him he’s better drop us a line. There isn’t very much to write since I last wrote. Wish I had a birthday presently for you – but wishes are not very substantial. Anyway I hope you keep real will till your next birthday and hope to see you before your next. Love to both of you, Ruby, Sam, and Gene ...

From: Ruby Chandler Mullholland, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Ruby Chandler Mullholland, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Wichita, Kansas on December 26, 1930 Mailing Address: Mrs. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois Return Address: Mrs. Sam Mullholland 427 E. Lewis Wichita, Kansas The wrapper on your box came from Mae. She sent me a coral necklace and hose to Sam and Gene. Wichita, Kansas December 25, ’30 Dearest Mama and Mart Since I have an hour or so left over I thought I’d get your letter answered today and let you know how we spent Christmas. We went down to Grove’s and Inez’s along about 10 am but had our own tree and gifts at home early this a.m. Inez had a small tree but we all exchanged candy except that they gave Gene gloves and hose. He also got 2 lovely pair of golf hose from Mae and Art and Bob gave him a dollar. He faired awfully well considering things. Sam gave me hose and I gave him a tie. We gave Gene a bugle, books and a drinking set. A girl here in town brought down a big box of chocolates with a note, “Mostly for Gene” on it. She came over to my house at El Dorado on Thanksgiving and ever since, she has been so generous. Kathryn Hutchinson is her name. I didn’t even send them a card. I didn’t miss very many though. I had a stack of them. An awfully nice one from Dr. Garrison. I noticed Mart had forwarded it from Pearl. Was glad to get a line from all the family so soon again. So I’ll write them all and then I’ll be all set to hear again. Don’t be surprised when I tell you to change our address soon. We aim to get our furniture over in about two weeks and move into a house in the 3700 block on North Lawrence. But I’ll let you know when we do. Just write here and I’ll drop a line later. I don’t know yet how long I’ll be at Dr.’s as there is such a slump in business. He told me to come back though and I noticed he paid off several others as he wouldn’t be needing them but of course even he doesn’t know how business will continue. Sam will go to work for an oil Co. here Jan 1. Don’t know his duties exactly – but its with oil rigs, etc. Which is something in our favor. We had a lovely dinner at Inez’s. Grove had bought a turkey and Bob had one given to him through the Sheriff so he and his girl and a boy friend also a friend of Grove’s besides us were there. Mae and Art didn’t come because they thought Dick was to be at home and and he was sent to Ottawa, Kansas on a job this a.m. so Mae’s plans were all broken into. They’re rather at sea anyway on account of Mother Davis being so low. She can’t get well now so its just a matter of how long her constitution can hold out. She surely is hearty – in frame that is – but has sinking spells and is bedfast. Inez has a colored girl so we didn’t have to do much all day. We had turkey with dressing, asparagus – buttered, mashed potatoes, giblet gravy with hard boiled egg diced in it, cranberry jelly, pumpkin pie, cake, coffee and rolls. We had fruit cocktail made of grapefruit, orange, pears, peaches, and some cherry in it. We also had waldorfaud salad. This cook had put a little sweet pickle in the waldorfaud and it was awfully good for a change. We had celery too. So we had such a big dinner and no work and they wouldn’t let me bring a thing. So it sure was easy on me. You know about how much time one has left when working. I made some candy last night and this a.m. and I’m going to mail a box to you and I was sorry not to get it to you before. I only sent Annie a story book but I think I can get a box of candy to her for New Year’s Day. I just write her address c/o of State Hospital, Dixon Illinois. I never have had a line from them either so a person never can tell whether she gets anything or not. I was so glad to hear of Lucerne’s baby doing better. I thought I might get a letter written to her too but owe one to Grace first, so I’ll try and get it written tonight. We have had such nice rather the past week but it looks different tonight, like it might snow. But I guess we might expect winter from now on. Gene will be out of school for about then days so he’ll get in a lot of play. He has two or three friends he visits back and forth with and I know their people are nice so I don’t worry about him just so I know where he’ll be all the time. He is awfully good about sticking around home and he reads a lot and does quite a bit around the house. He comes down to the store, runs errands and goes to a show now and then, so his time is pretty well filled up. Mr. Skaggs has had a serious attack of something like diabetes. He’s a man a great deal like Uncle Jim was and I wouldn’t be surprised if he didn’t go like he did. Mrs. Skaggs wrote me they will stay in Kansas City until he goes to hospital for a general exam. So I’ll have to write her soon again. You know we pay her storage for our furniture but I want it over here so bad and now think we can get a good house reasonable. Must quit now. Write soon. Hope all well. Lots Love, Ruby ...

From: Bess Chandler Roush, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Bess Chandler Roush, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Dunlap, Iowa on February 25, 1929 Mailing Address: Mrs. E.H. Chandler 937 Jackson St. Fredonia, Kansas Return Address: Mrs. J.W. Roush Dunlap, Iowa c/o Sam Mullholland Sunday 2 pm. Dearest Mamma and all of you, I was so glad to get your good letter but I thought you seemed a little homesick so though I’d do what I could to cheer you up. Of course by this time you’ll have Knox’s letter which will at least give you recent news of us. I had a letter from Lucerne a few days ago in which she said she had a letter from Mart saying he was feeling fine and hadn’t had a drink since Thanksgiving and was working at the A.P in Milwaukee a few hours a day or whatever time they needed him for. I thought it was so sweet of you to think of making something for Margaret and I think it would be lovely if you’d piece her a quilt. I’d send you the material and pattern if that wouldn’t be too much for you to do. The big day is to be June 9th and between now and then we are all going to be on the move. Aunt is quite a lot better now up all the time but not able to go yet or do anything. Friday afternoon John and I went in to see the Patriotic program in which Wayne participated. Then Friday night we (excepting Aunt) all went to Margaret’s Box Supper Program and Dance gel in her school. She had real good music donated but she didn’t make near as much money this time as she did in October. he coming marriage is beginning to seem more real all the time. She and her fiancé went to Ousha yesterday to purchase some of their furniture – a living room suite, one chair rug, bed room suite, and dinning room suite (six chairs, table, and buffet). They got so tired that they quit shopping and went to the Orpheum. Bill had the money with which to purchase the furniture and he is also buying teams harness and farm machinery and feed for stock – everything to farm with. He takes possession of the farm the first of March so he will batch until they are married. We are planning on a big wedding – or at least it sounds big to me. John and I are going to get silver like mine for her. Sure glad to get first hand information from the Mullhollands. Sounds as if they have a real thriving store. Knox and Wayne first left to go over to Hellers. They have a house pool table and all of them down to the four year old shoot pool. John has the contract to furnish the gravel for the new Catholic Church. They will haul 400 tons this coming week – all the work is to be donated – the tearing down of the old building and all the hauling and everything that can be done by the men. But they will have an architect design the building. The Hellers donated $1000 besides a lot of work. Margaret and I are embroidering a lot of things – last week we did a linen luncheon cloth 60×60 and six napkins – and we had just finished a bedroom set consisting of 2 long scarfs and one vanity set and pin cushions and now we are working on another set of pillow cases, dresser scarf, and vanity set. We have had some terribly cold weather. Last Friday morning the thermometer registered 5 degrees below zero and had been below zero most of the mornings in February. One month from last Friday will be Good Friday too and time to plant spuds. Can you believe it? Well, I reckon I’ve written everything. Maybe you’ve had letters from all by now. Lu is the only one I owe. Let me know if you want me to send the goods fro the quilt. Much Love, Bess ...
