From: Betty and Harold, To: Millie

From: Betty and Harold, To: Millie Monday Dearest Aunt Millie, Again I say I am sorry because I have waited so long to write. About August 10th I took Meiser to Colorado in order to avoid hay fever season in Kans. In less than a week after that I received a long distance phone call from Harold’s doctor in Hearth Platte, Nebraska telling me Harold was seriously ill in hospital with pneumonia. Louie’s wife went to Colorado to care for mother and I left Colo. for He. Platte and joined Harold. He was well enough to leave He. Platte in about three weeks. So we left and we came to Kansas City to see this Dr. Jere who operated on Harold five years ago. We were mistaken about Harold being able to make the trip to Kansas City so back to hospital we went again where he remained another two weeks. He is conversating now and Wed. of this week we go to Topeka for three weeks more of rest. Meier is being brought to Topeka today from Colorado. I am well considering every thing I have seen thru this summer. I do hope dear you are comfortable and that hay fever did not get you down this summer. Also hope your little dog is well and still with you as I know he is company for you. Bertie is busy as ever. Louie returned from Europe early last spring and will make no more trips abroad til peace is definitely declared over there which I fear is a long ways off. Weather here now is beautiful but we had a very hot summer as Ks. always does have. Hope to hear from you soon and that the report of you health will be favorable. Lots of love from both of us. Betty & Harold ...

From: Hattie, To: Mill

From: Hattie, To: Mill Dear Aunt Mill I know we should written sooner but just seemed like we have been so bust with this work all the time and my niece came here right after you were here. She was at the hospital taken treatment and I was there so much and her husband came every weekend and just seemed like there wasn’t any end to the different thing I had to see. I know you would like our house and when you come back be sure and come out to see us as we like to have you. I have had lots of house cleaning to do. I am about thru now. Sorry that I hadn’t let you know I had received the scarf and like it very much and I will inclose a dollar in the letter. Hope you are well and enjoying good health. Heaps of Love Write Soon Hattie ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Unknown

From: Martin Chandler, To: Unknown Mailed From: Unknown Business not very heavy just now. Don’t have very many freight trains. It’s getting pretty cold here. Have heavy frosts every night but during day it warms up so don’t need a fire. I get the Tribune regularly. Quite a but of surprising news. Didn’t think Geo Bixby would get married. Don’t see how he is going to support a wife. How did Ralph make out over stopping no 11 other morning? Gee they sure are strict on this road. Keep the merit and demerit system up all the time. Its called the brown record of discipline. When you get 90 demerits they let you go and no excuses go. I’ve got 10 for dating a train order 8th month in August instead of writing “August” out in full. Guess I’ve got them worked off by now though. They make a fellow toe the mark all around. They’ve eternally got spotters and assistants out on the line watching for every little thing. You can’t connect two words together in a train order not dt an “I” with a circle and all the words must be even distance apart. 2nd trick man here got 10 brownies account two words were too close together in a 19. They bring an order around some time and see if you have it on your file and if they don’t find it they want tot know why you haven’t filed copy of that order. Also send a man over road every month to examine all the operators on books of rules and time card. All the fellows say if you can work for the Southern Pacific you can work for any road in the country. It’s not hard work though, or at least it isn’t here. Don’t have any block wires to contend with. If you haven’t got anything for a train just give them the board. Don’t ever give the rans here the board though cause we’ve got a standing slow order for trains in both directions. Well guess I’ll stop and get busy. Got quite a few expense bills tonight. Hows Knox coming? Hope to hear from you all soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Unknown

From: Martin Chandler, To: Unknown Missing Page 1 Page 2: her today. That’s right Knox. You save all your letters-Ha. You all needn’t worry about the Mexicans hurting me. I’ve got a Negro gang here and have had ever since I took this camp. Besides all the Mexicans around here are all docile as a lamb. Sure is warm around here. I was in Denison today to do some shopping. Going in again day after tomorrow. Sure hope I get some more mail. Also go in Thursday, so can hold up some niggers checks. They ran off owing me $4.75 in commissary and I want to get it back if I can. The $4.75 will be taken out of my check for next half. I aint get a lick of sense. Think I’ll get most of it back though. Saw in today’s paper where U.S. was going to buy northern Mexico at cost of $300,000,000. Hope they do – There would probably be a lot of work going on, building railroads, etc. I’m going to stay until end of May so can get few clothes. I’m plume out of style with the duds I’ve got. I’ll get $5.00 day after tomorrow. Golly it’s sure hot. How’s Aunt Sarah and Uncle Jim? If I stay here until May I’ll be getting at least $35.00. That letter from Corbett was all right in a way. He said if I referred to them they would give same information as service letter would carry but didn’t say what kind of information a service letter would carry. That letter was from Herschberger anyway but I’m pretty sure it’s OK and will serve OK. when I look for job telegraphing. Gee Ralph will need his job telegraphing now wont he? Golly I weigh 140 pounds and never felt better in my life. Wish it wasn’t so warm. I’ve been taking 5 mile walk to Bona and back nearly every night. By Golly if the children don’t write pretty soon I’ll not address another letter to them. Knox do you hear that? Now if you want to save up some letters you had better get busy and write. Is Lucerne going to school in Morrisonville. Gee old Parish sure praises Dr. Thurmon in his paper each week doesn’t he? Gee I’ll bet Papa sure is busy now. Wish I was there to take few 31st. If I ever go through Kansas City I’m going get that service letter. Well guess I’d better close. 8:00 pm now and I want to cool off. All write. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Bess Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Bess Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Sunday Dearest Papa, Mama, and all, Sure do thank you for then five. Came in handy but I want you to know I earned the money to pay for my Xmas presents. Got $22 for my first case of nursing. No I didn’t either, Mae gave me $10.00 for staying with her. I didn’t want to take that but she just insisted and as I was altogether broke it looked awfully good. Then at Temps I stayed one week and 3 days for $22. Didn’t have anything to do but take care of the little girl. They treated me fine and invited me to stay over Xmas with them and eat turkey dinner but I wanted to eat with the girls. When I left the little girl I was nursing gave me an Xmas present. The sweetest little apron with tattling all around the the pieces set together with tatting wasn’t that nice? I have another place now out on College Hill. Three people down and I’ll get $30.00 a week here. I gave M & L $5.00 between them after Xmas. Lucerne is going to get her a pair of shoes. Margaret got hers and I got mine. They are awfully good looking. Our Xmas we sent home looked kind of slim didn’t it? I just had one day to shop and I had to wait until I made some money to shop with. I was picking out Xmas cards and found one like the one we sent home. I thought it had lots of good thoughts and it also there was a lot of loving thoughts with out gifts. Grace sent me a pair of silk stockings and Mae gave me a pair too. Mrs. D.D. gave each M, L, and I a drop de china handkerchief. They surely treated Ruby and Sam as well as the rest of us lovely- R & S were going to eat Xmas dinner over there and us girls over to Mae’s but we changed plans and we all ate over to Mary’s and such a dinner. We had a regular one – I’ll let Ruby tell you what she got. Ruby was awfully blue because Sam had to go back. Margaret has the flu and so far as I can hear is getting along fine. I have to hear thru Mae – She sent her thermometer down by Art. Mae said she would have taken care of Margaret rather than to have her down home at 215 E Dewey but she wasn’t strong yet herself. If Ruby and Lucerne need me I’m willing and ready to go but I thought $30.00 isn’t picked up every week. Do you hear anything from Martin? Bet he;s anxious to get home. Sure was glad to hear you were all better at home. Ruby, M, and L gave me a flowered petticoat and some handkerchiefs. Think I’ve told you everything Old Santa brought me now. I made Art and the boys a plate of homemade candy and have Mae a pair of Manicure scissors. When did the things arrive home? The girls hadn’t done any shopping to speak of and M and I did most of it Xmas Eve day. The day I went home from Temps. The baby is getting along fine and Ruby looks well. Hope to hear from you soon Love, Bess ...

From: Betty Ann Tilley, To: Amelia J. Wiggins

From: Betty Ann Tilley, To: Amelia J. Wiggins Dearest Aunt Millie, I have been away from home over a month as Harold had his vacation and we spent most of the time at his home in Catarissa Penna. We drove thru and even though it was twelve hundred miles it seemed like no trip at all. Your card was forwarded to me and as always I was glad to hear from you. I had hoped to get an apt. here but we have a vacancy in one of the apts. at some and besides mother is not at all well so tomorrow Harold is taking me to Topeka and at Xmas he will come to be with us. If at all possible I shall return here at that time with him. It is very difficult for me to leave mother at all and we can not always arrange to have her with us as it is not possible for her to have her comforts any place like at “Home Sweet Home.” I do hope darling you are well and have things comfortable. To be in a warm home is a lot to be thankful for, so many poor people suffer in cold weather. Speaking of weather, ever have we had such a fall even now winter has not arrived and its nearing Xmas. My little dog is sitting on the window sill looking down on the street, guess she thinks she is directing traffic as she growls when ever a dog passes or anything else she doesn’t like the looks of. Aunt Millie have you tried during the past few years to get glasses? Maybe you need new ones. It’s a shame for you not to have them if you could see better. Poor mother nothing helps her any, neither the operation nor glasses and it make me very sad. She still sees enough to get around the house and neighborhood but that is about all. I miss her and her interesting letters. I am trying to write larger so you can read my letter more easily. Do you ever hear from Lela? After all the money you spent on the child its pitiful that she isn’t with you when you do need her so badly. I wish it was so I could see you at least occasionally but I can scarcely leave mother to even be with my very good husband. I received a letter from Bertie yesterday. He keeps well but so busy he hardly has time to go see mother. His two younger daughters are away to school. One in Chicago and the other in Lawrence, Ks. He sees them both often as he has to make frequent trips to Chicago. I shall close now dearest Auntie and take my little dog out for a stroll. Again I shall say that I hope my letter finds you well and happy. Am glad you have so many good friends. Love and a kiss, Your devoted niece, Betty Ann Tilley Enclosed are a few stamps for your convenience. ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Albuquerque, New Mexico on March 6, 1892 European Hotel. Corner First Street and Railroad Avenue. Frank E. Sturges, Proprietor. Albuquerque, N.M. Mch 6th, 1892 My Dear Wife I will write you this morning. This is the first chance I have had to write any. I got here in the after part of last night. Am well and I trust this will find all of you well. I am awful anxious to hear from you but I can not say yet whether I will get to go to Prescalt or not. This being Sunday I have no chance to see any one so I can find out whether they will grant me a pass or not. This is a nice city and the people are very sociable. I am tired from riding on the cars. Did not get to sleep very much until this morning went to bed about three o’clock and sleep till eight. I saw some nice country coming down here and some very rough. It rained from the time I left K City Friday morning until some time in the night and then commenced S. morning and snowed until yesterday evening. There is some snow on the ground here and the mountains are wet with snow. The snow was 14 inches deep in Colorado and I thought some time the train would stop they went so slow. I can’t hardly describe how it looked going up the mountains. The train had two heavy engines and they did not go as fast as a man could walk. I will not try to give any further description of the country but will tell you when I come home. If I don’t get passed over the A & P I will not go to Prescalt. If I do get them I will telegraph to you so you can write to me. Everything here is high and I can’t afford to fool away money trying to dead head my way with train men. I would give any thing if I was with you and the children now. Kiss the children for me. Tell Vinnie I think of her too. I have written to you each morning since I left. I hope you are enjoying your self. I will close and write to you again tomorrow but not until often I find out what I am going to do. I will have to stay here tonight I guess. Good bye, From your Loving Husband, E.H. Chandler ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on July 24, 1887 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia. Freemaut Co Pearl, July 24, ’87 Dear Nettie Your kind letter of 21st. Just saw and read with great pleasure. I will try to answer this eve. I was in hopes the next letter I got from you I would hear you was going to be for home right away. Well two weeks want be very long to want because I am very anxious to see you. I went to the festival the other night and had a very good time but didn’t have any pictures. I am just about sick today and have been for two or three days. I was up till about one o’clock the night of the festival. Then Winslaw woke me at 2:30 to work for him. He came to St. Louis. So I had to work that day and till 12:15 the next night. So I guess that is what the matter with me. The men have had a bad time this pat day. It was raining and many of them were at work. So, the most of them got stuck or usual. This is a fine day and all the men are at work and I am glad of it. I get disgusted with lots of old drunken men. Well, I do wish you had come home in time to have taken sleigh ride. maybe it will snow again before winter’s close. If it doesn’t I expect it will snow next winter but I don’t like to wait so I’ll leave it at that. I am at a loss to know what to write today, nothing worth writing about. Oh, certainly I will excuse you for not answering that letter as you was busy and fixing to come home. But not for anything else. Well I guess I will have to close for want of something to write. But never mind I will pay up when you come home. Yes be sure to let me know when you are coming. I would be happy to come meet you. Hoping to heat from you again soon. I am Yours Truly, E.H. Chandler ...
