From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Eliojah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: El Paso, Texas on June 10, 1917 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Illinois St. Blanca January 10, 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all; Received 2 letters from Papa today. One via valentine and I was glad to get them both. Didn’t think you’d be able to get one to St. Blanca so quick. Nothing new to write. Getting along O.K. I called up both last night and asked them what they wanted me to do in case one called while I was working with other and they said just use my own judgement and if I pay no attention to them they get sore. I’m hitting the ball here pretty lively. Keep busy every minute of the night. The depo I trick open copied a 19 for a IP 2/5 train this pm to meet another IP No. 2 train at Fort Hancock. No. 2 got to Fort Hancock and waited 2 hours for the 2nd 5 to show up but they didn’t so he called up Dispr. on phone and went on. 2nd No. 5 was in a blind siding down the line. They claimed they didn’t get the order. Disp. came after open about it and open looked for his clearance and he had it non clearance so it will be up to the train creed. Well, must stop. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: England on January 9, 1917 Somewhere in England Jan 9th Dearest Folks, Thought I’d write few lines tonight while have time but can’t tell you much except that I’m feeling OK and had a pleasant voyage. It’s pretty cold here but not any worse than New Jersey. Am not allowed to mention hardly anything so don’ expect to learn anything about the war from me. Rode on an English train other night. They’re a great deal different from ours. They are built in compartments. Each compartment sealing 8 men (3rd class). No way of getting from one compartment to another except by getting off and entering another. Just like traveling in a carriage. Had a good stateroom on the steamer and didn’t get a bit seasick. I’ll write you again in few days and don’t worry if you don’t hear from me regularly. Hope you are all well- Lots Love Martin Address Exactly Like This: Martin W. Chandler Co B 126 Field Bin Dig Corps American Exp Forces ...

January 9, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Grace Chandler, To: Chandler Family

From: Grace Chandler, To: Chandler Family Monday 8th ’17 Dearest Folks Will try to write you a few lines this am. Was so glad to hear from Mama yesterday. Doesn’t matter to whom they are addressed, just so I get them. Am feeling much better, one breast is still swelled, and I have the night sweats and a breaking out on my body caused from fever, But I am getting stronger every day now. Mrs. Kelley left Saturday P.M. and I bathed the baby yesterday and this morning. She is doing fine and everyone was out yesterday afternoon, then Mr. and Mrs. Geo Maxon and son wee out last night. Guess we’ll keep having company, as so many have told Lorin they’re so anxious to see the baby. He’s going to try and get some pictures of her this noon. Thanks to Grandma Chandler, she has another silver dollar to add to her savings. Don’t think she should have done it, but we appreciate it. Lorin took her little bank up to the bank this A.M. He adds something every day I think. Mrs. Klingner and Mella were out after you left Mama and brought her a little solid gold hand ring. The material Ruby has for her coat is so pretty and I’m sure proud of it. Guess you’d be surprised, as we were shocked to hear Mr. Bartlett the Wabash agent here, committed suicide in the depot on Friday. No reason has been assigned to his rash act, but an auditor came in to check him up and a dray man testified at the coroners inquest that he had heard them arguing over some freight bills. Some additions that had been added to Paddock Lumber Co.’s bills. But other people think it was family affairs. Will send you the clippings from the papers if I can find them. He was the agent here for 7 years. It certainly was a shock to everyone as they were supposed to have been the happiest of families. Am so glad to have Ruby here. She is so good to me, but I’ll tell you I missed Mama and so did Mrs. Kelley. She spoke of you so often. Bess was so sleepy after talking all night that she layer down and slept in the afternoon and I had a lump in my throat all day. Ruby had spells over the “offspring” when she arrived and has never quite gotten over her yet. Wish everyone of you could see her. Lorin is going to be in a minstrel show tomorrow night in Taylorville , some fellows pulled out at the last minute and they asked Lorin to take one place. He’s to be an end man and has a prominent part. He’s studying his song “Are you from Dixie” and his gags. Either Bess or Ruby will go to Taylorville and see it. Wonder what causes Mama to get so dizzy? Mrs. Kelley had those kind of spells. Yes, we let Mrs. Wilson know you had gone. She was called to St. Louis on Friday to take care of her niece, a trained nurse, who has pneumonia and expects will be gone for some time. Mrs. Peuce (your advisor Mama) is also very ill with pneumonia. They have a trained nurse to care for her. Other box from fathers came after such a time. He sent the baby a powder puff, me a small picture of mother and a baby, a towel, and a box of stationary, and a lot of Christmas candy. Was nice of him to remember us. He sent each member of his family something. We heard from mother , three miners and she have taken more cold and was having lots to do. Bess and Lorin went to Bartlett’s funeral. It as held at the house hey said. There were oceans of flowers. Will be glad to get Lucerne’s drawings . Was so sorry to her Mary school her, but its all over now and it didn’t hurt anyway did it Lucerne? When little Jack was here, he went over to Waggners and told them we had a dog. They said “what kind of dog is it?” he said he didn’t know. They asked if it was a bird do. He said, “No! This dog don’t fly!” Ha. Mrs. Waggner and Gladys came over the next day after you left. We have had lots of company. Lorin just called up from town and said I had letters from home, but he had to go to Taylorvillle and won’t be back until night. Said he’d send the letters down to the school house to Bess and I’ll get them about 4 pm. I want to send this up by Bess this noon so I’ll close for this time as she’s ready to do back. Love to Everybody, Grace ...

January 8, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Sierra Blanca January 2, 1917 Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Sierra Blanca Jan 2, 1917 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all, Got in here last night and went to work. Job is enough to keep a man busy but not so awful hard. Have a night helper to handle baggage, etc. So I don’t have to do anything but telegraphy and clear trains on Both TP na dishes. I’m plumb disgusted with board here. This place is about the size of Hillview and only one place in town to board and they sure treat a fellow awful. Have to carry wash hater to your room and no carpet and just about the poorest accommodations a man could find. Got 1 pork chap, 2 biscuits and a few friend potatoes for breakfast and thats all. I got up from the table hungry. Charge 30.00 month. I went to bed right after breakfast this morning and got up at exactly 10 minutes after seven and they told me I was too late for supper. Had to go buy a meal at Brown’s News. They know they have a fellow cinched and they don’t care how they treat them. I asked how much for just a room and they said just the same as for board and room. Now what do you think of that? I was figuring on getting my meals at restaurant but they queered that. It’s just plain hold up. They know you can’t get any place else to stay. If I stay here I’m sure going to get the Co to give me a box car or else build some kind of shanty and go to Baching. Can’t possibly get by for less than 35 to 40 a month for board the way it is now. If I get up after supper time I have to buy a meal and its hard to arrange sleep so can get breakfast and supper both cause I generally sleep 10 hours. Here I never eat more than 2 meals a day and they wont give me one of those if I happen to be late but they charge for it. Just the same – I told the manager what I thought abut it and he informed me I had the privilege of going some place else to board if I didn’t like it, which of course he knew I couldn’t get any place else. I’m going to every house intern tomorrow and see if I can’t get a room. Job pays 85 don’t get the 5.00 for handling mail as the night helper does that. Have lots trains to clear. Just twice as many as at Valentine cause here we get the Tp. trains too but don’t mind it as long as don’t have to handle baggage and mail. Pretty cool here. They say they have cold winters here. Well its now 11:45 s will stop and get things lined up to go to work. Work from 12 to 8. Love to all, Martin ...

January 2, 1917 · Post Cards

From: Bess Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler and Family

From: Bess Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler and Family Mailed From: Morrisonville, Ill on November 6, 1916 Mrs. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill 1:40 Sunday PM Dearest Mama, Papa, and all I’ve just gotten cleaned up and thought I had better write. We had intended to go to Taylorville to a football game but something was the matter with the car. I did want to go so bad, but I’m going to Jane Carrie’s house to see about a party the Glee Club is to give Thursday night. I got along all OK with my recitation. I’ve had so many compliments on it. Nearly every time I’d see anyone who was there, they’d tell me how well I did. You know I told you that some Taylorville Ladies came down and organized a Hilson Ladie’s Club. Well Mrs. Pence was appointed as a committee of one to arrange for a Glee Club. She asked me and six other girls and we sang here, at Harvel, and at Taylorville. Our songs were real cute. We had one to the tun of My Little Girl – Oh Mr. Hughes you’re stock are dropping You are losing notes each day Oh Mr. Hughes we hear you’re knocking Tho you’re many miles away We read your talks in all the papers What you’ve promised not to do Oh Mr. Hughes we know you’re lonely But we can not note for you We had lots of other ones too and some Wilson yells. While we were at marvel a man gave us five dollars, so were going to have a party on it. We get to invite “our company” – Don’t know who to invite. The ladies of Tayloville treated us so nice and altogether we’ve had lots of fun. We’ll know Tuesday who is to be our president. I got my coat while I was at Taylorville. It’s brown velour. I did want a plush with brown fur trimming but they were too expensive. Hope I get to come home Thanksgiving but guess I wont if I don’t get a pass because my fare would be nearly six dollars. I’ll sure be home Xmas tho’ and get to stay from Dec 22 to Jan. Hope you are all well – I’m getting along fine at school. We had a program in my room day before yesterday. It was real good. The girls asked me to sin so I sang Little Gray Home in the West. We had company, too. Lorin has just offered Grace and I $0.15 each if we read something against Wilson in the Republican news paper. You couldn’t pick up a republican news paper without reading something against Wilson. In fact that’s about all it contains. I’m enclosing a five with which I want to pay my lodge dues for the past four or five months. Guess it wont come amiss if everything is as high as it is here. Flour is $3.00 a sack. You never did say whether or not you got the other $5.00 (which I borrowed to buy shoes in Springfield) I returned but I guess you did because you got the letter. I’ve got all of my debts paid now, Pearl Store, my board, Donohoo’s, Ruby, and you. I wrote to Mrs. Moultrie too – All I owe, now is a letter to Ruby and I’ll try to write it soon. Martin never did answer my letter. When you write tell him I’m looking for one. Lot’s of Love, Bess ...

November 6, 1916 · Post Cards

From: Grace Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler and Family

From: Grace Chandler Gourn, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler and Family Mailed From: Morrisonville, Illinois on October 27, 1916 Mrs. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Mrs. L.E. Gourn Morrisonville, Ill Morrionville 10/27 Dearest Folks, Rec’d Mama’s letter this am addressed to me and tho’t I’d write this after noon while I am resting. Washes pretty good washing this am. It is a fine bright day to dry them. Didn’t think when I got up I wanted to wash but everybody else around me was washing and just hated to see them get ahead. So Lorin stayed at home and helped me and with my electric washer and ringer was all thru by 10:30. Just got up at 7:30. Wasn’t that going some? But after I get all the scrubbing done I am pretty tired. Lorin went in the car to Raymond this afternoon and to Nokomio if he can make it. He will be back tonight. Sat we cleaned the house all up and washed windows inside and out. I don’t think I’ll do any general house cleaning. Washed the wood work around the windows and am going to rent a vacuum sweeper (electric) to clean the rugs and mattresses. All of us, including Glenn, Thelma, bots, Callie, Allie and Chas were at Mother’s for dinner and supper yesterday. Had dandy meals and a good time. Bess and I went uptown Sat pm to mail that mail you received and went on down to Callie’s and she insisted on us staying for supper. Lori came home on the 6:30 train and came on down there. We stayed until 11:3. Mother send me a bunch of green tomatoes today so I’ll have to work them into pickles this week. She made some dandy chow chow. Have brot my flower baskets inside. Think I’ll take out some of the best plants and pot them. Haven’t much room tho for flowers. That is, not a good sunny window to keep them in. Lorin got me a nice little trimmed oak sewing chair which is very comfortable. He bronzed my old gray shoes until now they look like new. What is the matter with Mr. Hanks that he has to be operated on? That’s too bad, hope he gets better. Hope Martin gets to stay at the place where he is all winter. He seems to be doing pretty well. Wonder why Bill Watters sold out in the picture show biz? Thot it was such a paying proposition? Maybe Annie was jealous of he and Roy being in together. Ha. Glad Ruth adn Basil have gone back . The course of true love never did run smooth. Would like for her to come up and visit us. I know Bess would enjoy having her. Bess wore her new dress, hat, and shoes yesterday and looked so nice. She’s so slender. Ha. I can’t help but notice it. Lorin has a nice new brown overcoat. He may be over there soon. I know he is going to make the territory around there either this week or next. Wish we could come home Thanksgiving. I expect Bess will if she gets enough days off. I want her to so she can visit with you all and then tell me all about it. Well this is all I can think of for now. Was glad to get Mama’s letter. Hope her cold is better. Would like to get weighed, believe I am getting heavier. Bess, Lorin, and I walked up town yesterday morning before going down to mothers. Much love to all. Write soon, Grace ...

October 27, 1916 · Post Cards

From: Grace Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Grace Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Sent From: Morrisonville on October 1, 1916 Morrisonville 10/1/16 Dearest Papa, Mama, & all: – Guess you think it’s about time I was writing – Bess and I just got back from Sunday School. We took a spurt this am and got ready and went to the Presbyterian SS and everyone treated us so nice we are glad we went. bess is correcting some papers and making out some examination papers and I have dinner on cooking. Did think I would come in and play the graphonolia but thot I would be making better use of my time if I write home. Ruby left yesterday morning and we sure miss her. Bess and i took her to the train, had to get up at 6 am so we could have breakfast and get to the depot by 6:58 am. Bess and i then came home, cleaned the house real good and she had to go to a teachers reading circle at 2 pm. I dusted and scrubbed after she left took a bath and dressed up again and went to meet her. Got there in time to hear part of the work. We walked up town again went to Callie’s and she insisted on us staying and having supper. So Lorin came in on the 6:30 pm train and we went to meet him and all took supper there. Then went to the picture show. Lorin bought me the most beautiful purple velvet tricornered hat for my birthday, he said, but that isn’t until the 6th of next month. So he was in plenty of time – Ha. My white coat just got back this am from the cleaners, so you make know I wore my new hat and white coat to S.S. and felt quite dressed up. Bess cleaned her lack suit yesterday and wore my other purple hat. Our electric washer came yesterday pm and Lorin got up early this morning and had it in working order by the time we got up. It sure us a peach and I’m tickled we have it – will sure save my back. Lorin is going to Taylorville to get out car today, had to leave it up there the last time he was up there, on account of some man running into him and busting it up some. Will cost about $30 to get it fixed up again. Lorin sued him for $200 – the trail comes up next week. Hope he gets what it will cost us. The car is alright now so he is going to get it today. I am cooking apples whole not peeled, with such red skin, also sweet potatoes, kraut pork & wieners. Have raw tomatoes with mayonnaise dressing for dinner. How is papa? You said he was not feeling well when you last wrote. Hope is better by now. Guess Margaret & Lucerne will have a great time in St. Louis. Lorin said I could go, but I don’t believe I want to. It would be different if he and Bess could both go. I was surprised Leta & John stayed in Pearl to spend their honeymoon. Guess they have gone to St. Louis by now. Mon. P.M. Guess I’ll finish this and get it mailed. After dinner yesterday we drove to Taylorville with Oren Bloxom and another fellow, attended a football game, and drove our own car back home. Oren Bloxom came back with us – He’s a Taylorville boy, and is going to be a lawyer. Bess entertained him until his train time. I was so tired, Lorin and I went to bed. I had lunch for all. Lorin forwarded two letters to Ruby this a.m. I told him to send them to Pearl I was afraid she wouldn’t get them if we sent them to Granite City. I pulled the pears, Ruby will be glad to know and have just finished two gallons of pear butter. I had them on hand for over two weeks, so you may be sure I’m glad they’re cooked up. Lorin is going to put up the base burner this afternoon. It isn’t very cold yet but he thought he’d better put it up while he had time. Bess said at noon that she must write home. I told her I had a letter started. She has four sets of papers to correct this evening after she comes home. She’ll be pretty busy this week with giving examinations and correcting papers. I wish papa could come up if only for a few days, might do him good and we would be tickled to death to have him. Some of you could get a pass and come up anytime. Well this is all I can think of for now. Lorin will be down around that part of the country next week, I suppose. Write when you can, we’re always ticked to hear from home. Much love to all, Lovingly, Grace Tues. A.M. – Am sure going to get this mailed this morning. We have a train out of here for St. Louis at 3:37 P.M. and Lorin was just saying at the breakfast table that we could go down and see the Veiled Prophet tonight and get home on the midnight train if Bess could get her Supt. to teach for her half hour. So no telling we may be there tonight ourselves. The matter is to be settled at noon after Bess talks with the Supt. and Lorin is to study it over. Write soon. Grace ...

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Ruby D. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Ruby D. Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on September 29, 1916 Mailing Address: Miss Ruby D. Chandler 2224 B. St. Granite City, Illinois Return Address: PO Box 101 Pearl, Illinois Pearl, Illiois, Spetmber 29, 1916 My Dear Ruby: Just now I heard the honk, honk of an auto in front of the door but just thought it was Lewis. Then I asked Lucerne to look out and see, and it was the Smarts of Milton come down to meet May, and May was with him. She said Vinnie had sent her word to come at once as Lisa was so much worse. She asked for you and wanted you to go up with her. I wish you had been here, for it seems that none of us has been of any assistance to them. May then asked if I could jump in and go as you was not here, but of course I couldn’t leave just now, and I feed so bad about it too. And Dr. is away in hospital too, and I guess that makes Lisa feel that there is no one to look out for him. I would liked to have heard how Uncle Jim is today. Mr. Johnson was asking me how he was, and said if it hadn’t been for Aunt Sarah he wouldn’t have been down there. I told him that was the place for him for there were so many of them all there and so good to take care of him. He said if he didn’t change his mind he was going down there next Monday. We washed today and I am tired out. Didn’t get done till after noon. I hope Aunt Sarah is feeling all right. Annie did not say in her letter but I guess if there had been anything the matter with her, she would have mentioned it. I am making corn salad now just started on it about those little onions I wrote you about. I wouldn’t get them if they are a big price. 40 per gallon is plenty to pay for them. I must stop now. Love to all, Mamma ...

September 21, 1916 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Santa Margarita, CA on September 20, 1916 M.W. Chandler Southern Pacific Company Santa Margarita, Cal. Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill. Pike Co. Santa Margarita, Spet. 20 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all, Received a letter from Papa yesterday and a long on today and was sure glad to get them. Gee they sure are doing some business back there. Think I told you in other letter I’d be here a month longer and possibly two. Glad to hear Ralph is back at work. I don’t want to work on era very bad but I’d like awful well to be closer to home. Gee I wish I could see some of you . I sold a ticket to Waterburg Conn last night – $72.40- I didn’t have any ticket on hand so I wired Sam Luis Abirro to send me one and gave him the routing and he sent it up all made out. Excursion tickets are on sale the year round. Every Friday and Saturday have reduced rates to nearly all the places along the line. Can get a ticket to Chicago and return for $65.00. Sure have some powerful engines on this line. They’re all oil burners. Haven’t a coal burner on the line. I stuck a first class passenger train 5 minutes yesterday morning account putting “9th” for “September” – They might give me 10 days for it – They’re awful strict. They all say that if you can work for the S.P. you can work for any road in the country. They sure enforce their rules. I was in a hurry to make out the clearance as train was ready to leave and without thinking I put 9/20 instead of September 20 – That’s OK on any other road but this one. It takes about 3 years to land a job on this road. But if extra work is good I think I’ll stick. Of course I’d probably lay off and come home before 3 years. But these are pretty good jobs and I want to land something steady if I can. I don’t have to fill lamps or lanterns or sweep out or handle baggage or anything like that. Have a baggage boy to do that. All stations have a baggage boy. All I have to do is sell tickets, copy orders, and put on mail. Sure copy a bunch of orders and seek quite a few tickets. Well must stop. Hope to hear from you all soon- I’ll write more often Lovingly, Martin ...
