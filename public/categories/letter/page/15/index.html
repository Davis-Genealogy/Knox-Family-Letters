From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: France on March 10 France Mar 10th Dearest Folks, Will write just few lines tonight while have good chance. Nothing new to write. Have received no mail yet. Suppose it will be several months before you can get a letter to me at this address. Requires quite a while to get an answer to a letter. Have moved since last writing. Would like to tell you of our locations. Things are pretty warm here. Been having some very nice weather here for past week. Hope it continues. I’ve been feeling very good. Get plenty to eat. Have a pretty good YMCA here. Affords a good place to write letters. Well must close and turn in. Am on K.P. now and have to get up at 4 am. Hope you are all feeling well. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin P.S. Dont have any of my letters put in “Tribune.” ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: France on February 28 Dearest Folks, Have been traveling about quite a bit since last writing you. Was transferred to the 117th F.B.S.C. Feb 12. Like the organization very well and if I had my mail, clothes, etc from the 1st FB would just as soon stay with this organization. My service record is still with my old company. Don’t know when I’ll get a pay. Haven’t received any pay since October. Would like very much to get the mail you’ve already sent to me at 1st FB. But don’t know whether or not it will be forwarded here. Been having some very rainy weather the past week. Guess this is the rainy season. First I’ve seen since being over here. Would like to tell you of our whereabouts but of course that’s forbidden. Am feeling very good. Only hope you are all feeling as well. Must stop for this time. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin Address: Pvt. Martin W. Chandler Co ‘C” 117 F.B.D.C. A.E.F. Via New York ...

February 28, 1900 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Santa Margarita, California M.W. Chandler Santa Margarita, Cal 8034 Santa Margarita Mr. E.H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike Co Dearest Papa, Mama, and All Received letter from Papa yesterday and sure glad to get it. I’ve been working 2nd trick for last 2 days. 2nd trick man laid off and man they sent to relieve him couldn’t handle 2nd account so much clerical work to do. Don’t think he can handle 3rd either. Agent wired them that he was putting me on 2nd. Gee I have lots of clerical work to do. Have at least 10 car loads of wood, ore, and apples, each day. Ore all goes to Niagara Falls. Costs $750.00 per car to ship it. Have both Ford and Reed abstracts to make out, damage reports, write up freight bulls in cash book, have to make out Western Union and Tuckit report at end of month. Work from 4 pm to 12 but generally come to work at noon. Came over at noon today and made out ticket report and Western Union up to date. So wouldn’t have so much to do tomorrow. I’ll be on this job for about a week longer and when regular 2nd tick man comes back agent said he would see that I got the 3rd trick back. He came over to hotel and awakened me and asked if I would work 2nd trick. I like the job OK. Get lots of good sleep now. I have 3 passenger trains to sell tickets for and both locals and 2 regular freight trains on this trick but don’t have to get outside of office. Local conductors unload freight and wheel it in warehouse and seal cars and baggage boy handle the passenger trains and check baggage. All I have to do is make out switch lists and clear them. Have quite a few orders but not as many on 3rd trick. Both locals get along between 10 and 11 pm. Only thing I don’t like about this job is having to work evenings. I’ve been going up to see a bunch of school teachers every night and have lots of fun. Two of them are Catholic and I get into some awful arguments with them some times. All nice girls though. Always have some candy made or a bunch of fruit. Have a swell Victrola and two of them play the piano. How’s Knox getting along in school? Is Uncle Jim any better? Hope Aunt Sarah is ok. Well must stop and get busy. Hope you all write soon. Lucerne I haven’t heard from you for a long while. Guess Hipockets is waiting till he learns to spell before he writes. Ha. Will write again soon. Lovingly, Martin ...

January 27, 1900 · Post Cards

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler and Family Mailed From: Ahrweiler, Germany on January 26 Ahrweiler, Germany Jan 26 Dearest Papa, Mama, and all Received letter from Papa day before yesterday dated Jan. 1st and very glad to get it and to know everybody is well. Was very anxious to know that Mama and Anna had recovered from the attack of influenza. Think I mentioned in a previous letter that I had received a bunch of my back mail including letters from Ruby, Grace, and Lorin. Not very much news to write. Persistent rumors indicate that we will leave next month. Most of the rumors sound very plausible and I’m beginning to put a little faith in them. Certainly would love to get home. I’m still on the same job. Business has been rather heavy last few days. Was notified few days ago that I would attend Liaison School at Coblens but have heard nothing definite about it since so don’t know whether or not I’ll go. Hope so anyway. The people here are as cordial as ever. They don’t seem to be suffering in the least. All the families I’ve seen so far have had plenty of food. I ate supper with a German family (Place where I room) other night and they had roast pork, something I hadn’t tasted for a year. Has been pretty cold last few days. Has snowed several times lately but not heavily. Having quite a bit of entertainment through the Red Cross, K of C and YMCA shoes have been pretty good. The 2nd Canadian Division met the 42nd (Rainbow) Division in an athletic contest short time ago. We won the meet by quite a margin. I attended the boxing bouts. We won 5 out of the 6 matches, scoring two knockouts. Had a very large attendance. Well must close for this time. Want to write to Grace tonight. Wrote to girls at Wichita several days ago. Hope this finds you all well. I’m feeling fine and dandy. Write often. Lovingly, Martin ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Grace Chandler

From: Martin Chandler, To: Grace Chandler Mailed From: France on January 22 France Jan 22nd Dearest Grace and all, has been some time since I’ve written so thought I’d write few lines this pm while nothing to do. Had a pleasant voyage across the Atlantic. Was on the water Christmas and New Years. Am in the Hospital here now with measles. Come in the 15th. Am getting along OK and feel good. Hows Audrey getting along? My I’d love to see her as well as rest of you. It hasn’t been very cold here past 10 days, but has rained considerably. I expect to join my company in about a week. Well I can’t say very much so will close. Be sure and write soon as you can. Hope you are all feeling well. Lovingly, Martin Address: Pvt. M.W. Chandler Co “B” 1st Fed Bn St. Corpo. American Evped. Forces Via New York ...

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Martin Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Little Silver, New Jersey Mr. E. H. Chandler Pearl, Ill Pike Co 5th Dearest papa mama, and all Received 2 letters from papa day before yesterday. Awful glad to get them. Was just starting home from club and thought would drop you couple lines right quick. Nothing new to write. Don’t know when we will leave. Nobody seems to know. May be here until Christmas and again may leave within the week. We’re all prepared to leave immediately. They fixed up the allotment for me today. You will get 20.00 about 1st of Nov. and each month from then on for 1 year. I wont have 20 myself to send it home now. Seems like it slips through my fingers just as I get it. Hope everybody is well. Am in hurry just now so will stop and write more some other time. Feeling fine. Lovingly, Martin ...

January 5, 1900 · Post Cards

From: Ruby Chandler, To: Manca

From: Ruby Chandler, To: Manca Monday A.M. Dearest Manca, I will write just a note at this time and enclose any letters that have come since I wrote last. Bess said she’d write herself today so she can tell you anything I have left out. Bess came yesterday (Sun.) noon and Papa was at home too so we had a good visit. She looks fine. She brought each of us something new and brought you a new dress which we’ll make and send you. I want you to visit until your pass limit and rest assured that perhaps all things wont go to “rack and ruin.” Anyway we might get some sewing done for you under stress of your necessity especially while visiting and you know how much that knowledge of necessity can cause us to accomplish at times so we’ll try to get another little package to you before long. I’m sure I appreciate and thank Aunt Annie’s kindness in giving you such a fussy dress. That’s just like her. I was talking to several down at the soldier’s banquet about Uncle’s death and they all had praises for Aunt Annie. Clem Hanback talked as if she Aunt had been her bosom friend. I’ll send you the papers, too. Martin sent his card here for registration as you’ll see by his letter. Papa had the garden in perfect condition also the lawn and has poled the beans, latticed the tomato plants and planted radishes. Annie has been all I could wish for the past several days. Love to all. Lovingly, Ruby ...

January 1, 1900 · Post Cards

From: David Knox, To: Annie

From: David Kno, To: Annie To Annie Dear Annie, It’s true I have not been writing to you much, since Nettie went up there knowing that you had a chance to hear from some of us pretty often. Would say we were sorry to hear of you being somewhat under the weather but hope ere this you are all right. Bad colds are very common now everywhere. The weather being so changeable no doubt is the cause. Your Ma and I have both had colds and Johnie had a fearful cough at night especially. He is now working everyday now on the section with Honan. Seems to like it very well of course. Mr. Honan is a good friend of his which is some difference. Boys must not have sister do too much and he is all the boys. You tell him I am having this boy in training. He says he know he can output him because he don’t fall down. We are not getting impatient but ply want to see you a lll. Still more so as the expected time draweth near, I see no reason why George cannot come. It is only awhile spent and we all have to submit to losing time if any of us get sick. Try to get him to come. I like the tone of your last letter. It sounds as though you are coming some time , as I said, no use in waiting to get ready. Maggie is now making the machine work and has been for some days, so she will be in practice and have the machine in good order. Maggie is a machinist . She has just finished a dress for her Ma, Lizzie, and herself by some help from Lizzie F. Se says she will teach you all some new styles after you get here. I I suppose the children will be almost as big as she is, as she does not grow a bit. Still she is real handy to do anything she undertakes to do. I have not had a letter from Willie for some 10 days or more. Suppose he is well. He spoke of having bad colds-this is now a fair day. This after noon, but oh how muddy was the slush and snow. Nettie say you have got very big. That will be some surprise to us to see how having got so fresh. Iowa soil must be healthy for you folks as Nettie looks much fresher than when she left. I mean in her posture. Willie talked some of going west. Do you know anything about it? He merely mentioned that he had some notion to do so, but did not say when or positive about it. I will now close as the evening meal is about here and want to send this out. Respects to you all. Hoping to see you all soon in good health and in good spirits. I am as ever your Pa- David Knox ...

From: Jesse, To: Margaret

From: Jesse, To: Margaret Fri Eve 5:40 P.M. At Home Dearest One, As I feel so lonely this P.M. thought I would write you as I haven’t seen you for two or three evenings. That is to converse with you. Sorry it was so that we could not be together last eve. Trusting it will so be I could see you at Sunday School next Lord’s day if not before, trust it will be before. Was up past the house last PM. But could not see you – yourself and your sister’s family’s my sincere heartfelt sympathy in your and their trouble. Trusting the little one will continue to get better. Sorry dear you thought I was mad the eve we were conversing at a distance I sure was not and Margaret I am more than willing to say please a thousand times for you and also do anything I can for to please you.Dearest I will be up by the house at 8 o’clock P.M. and if you can see me a moment would be a pleasure to me. But if not will wait til it is convenient. Trusting you will not think I have advanced a step that I had ought not to of done by writing this. But dear when I do not get to see you regularly I am lost – I don’t know what I will do when you go to the city. Margaret – take good care of yourself and do not get sick – as I know you are worn out for you was up all night you must retire early this P.M. and get your regular rest. Will Close By By From your own Boy Jesse ...
