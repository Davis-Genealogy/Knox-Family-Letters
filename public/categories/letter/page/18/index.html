From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on December 28, 1886 Sidney, Ia. Dec. 28th ’86 Mr. E.H. Chandler Dear Lige: – You’re most welcome letter received yesterday eve late. I was very glad to hear from you again and to heat of you all begin well as this leaves us all at present. Oh! I know you all had a better time there, Christmas than I did here, I think the tree must have looked awful pretty and I only wish I could have gazed upon it. Oh! I am so tired of staying here and last night after I had retired I was thinking of home and nearly made up my mind that I would write you today to say that I would start for home soon and you could write and tell me where you would meet me, but this morn I thought I would stay a while longer anyway. I would hate to leave Johnnie, and especially as he has not found anything to do, he would be so home-sick if I would go home and Willie gone to. I don’t know when Johnnie does intend to go home. I have not heard him say anything about it only to wish he was there. I think Maggie’s got an awful nice present, also Ma, from the tree. Yours is quite nice also. Who was so thoughtful as to put those on for you? Do you know, I received another present since I wrote you your last. Mr. Hill sent me a large breast pin and a note asking me to accept of it with his best wishes. It is real pretty I think. I hope you and Maggie has received your presents before this and I hope your cup or saucer was not broken when it reached you. Lige please excuse this short and poorly written letter as I will have to hurry to get it in the Riverton mail now and I am going to call at the doctor’s office to have him to do some thing for my face and hands. There is some kind breakout on them and I sure am uneasy about it. Hoping to hear from you soon. ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on December 4, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co. Pearl Ill Dec 4th ’86 Dear Nettie, I will try and answer your kids letter that I received yesterday. I was truly glad to hear from you as I am always thinking what day I will get a letter from you but this time I am afraid it will be a good while for I have not answered the other letter I received 3 or 4 days ago. I will ask you to excuse me this time and I’ll promise not to do so anymore. I have been so busy I did not have time to write. The river has froze over and all the freight comes by rail. I had 15 thousand pounds freight to take care of yesterday and had the crew of the Mattie Bell here. They all went to St. Louis. Just fourteen number. There are lots of trains running and I am trying to get out my reports now. So you can see that I have been usually busy. Yes I am sure you will enjoy yourself while Johny is there till I hope he will find a good situation for the winter. Yes I think most any thing will beat railroading. We had an election on the 1st of this month. Sam McKee Flatan and Will Clauinger Flatan. Got 9 Votes. McKee 47 and Clauinger 48. I do not know where George Steelewell lives. His wife is at home but George has skipped out. I suppose you have heard of his trouble have you not. Every body is having a good time in Pearl. Frank Wood is in town I think. Lizzie J is having a good time. There hasn’t been anything new in Pearl since I wrote to you last. If you was here I could tell you lots but I can’t write it. Haven’t been at church for a week. There is church, a green pond tonight if you was here we would just got flying as there is a good snow on the ground. I can not write a very long letter this time but the next time I will not be so busy then I will write you a long letter if can think of enough to write. Winslow and get a long bass. He is awful good to help. Now Nettie I must close. Have a hard days work before me hoping to hear from you soon. I Remain Yours Truly E.H. Chandler ...

From: Linnie Samuels, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Linnie Samuels, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: St. Louis, Missouri on November 22, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Iowa Freemaut Co. Riverton, Iowa September 7th, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox My Dear Friend, I will now attempt to answer your most esteemed letter which I received Saturday morning. I was very glad to hear from you and that you were thinking of returning to Iowa, and also to hear of your brother. The Riverton young people miss him so much and now they are very lonesome without Miss L. Ellis and mr. Talbott to liven up the parties for they made everything lively when they were about. I am all alone today. Mamma went to Hamburg yesterday and today she has gone over to Mrs. Thompson after some plums and I stayed home to cook dinner for some men that were working here. Brother Joe and Mr. Wes Cowlas came down from Sidney today and they said everybody were well there. Miss Nettie – Among the first you go to visit must be myself. That is, if I do not go off to school before you come. When you come I will have a party while you are here and have the Sidney and Riverton young folks come and those you know at hamburg. You may bet we will have a fly time while you are with one. I ask your pardon for not answering sooner for Sunday. I had company all day and yesterday I was making a dress. Misses Katie and Crissie Thompson started for Lexington, Mo. to attend school and Miss Jennie began to teach school yesterday and they feel very lonesome without thereon during the evening. Jennie teaches in the Riverton school. It is getting very late and I will have to close with my very best regards to you and all the family. I remain Your True Friend Linnie S. ...

November 22, 1886 · Post Cards

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox CHandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on November 14, 1886 Pearl, Ills Sunday Nov 14th 1886 Dearest Nettie, Your kind letter of 11th received, will answer this morning. I received your letter the next day after you wrote it but thought I would wait til Sunday to answer. It’s getting so cold can’t write after night very well. I was at Church last night. This will be the last night if they don’t get any converts. Two weeks last night since they commenced they have got one to join the church and two more. I guess you loam up in grand style with that new coat. Better kook our or you might out grow it. Then you would have to buy another one but you could come to Pearl and get small again. I don’t know how I am weighing now aint had any scales all winter. Have to guess at everything I ship. Sink & Shea got back from their 2nd trip to Pittsfield last night. I do not know how they made it but they were under 15 hundred dollars last account. Your Pa got home Friday night. Don’t think Shea done any thing with the report I told you about in my last letter. The Sheriff came to Pearl last night after eve. I don’t know who he was after. He stayed all night at your house. Millie Rose is in Pearl again. HS mad a mash at the night Opr. Mr. Wheeling is here with a lot of old goods selling at Auction for man by name of Ladow. Pusby Stephouson has made a solid match on the Milliner’s Miss Hormidy. Alex Stephouson has took John Myriser’s place selling fruit trees. Belly Maynard is working on the bridge. Him this girl has played out again. Lizzie Frazier is up at Mr. Hess’ yet. This is about all that I know to write this time. Am having a very good time going to Church but don’t get away from depot til dark every night. I wish you was at home then. I think I would have a good time. I know I would. Do you have any idea what month you will come home in. Write soon. Regards to all Yours Truly E.H. Chandler ...

November 11, 1886 · Post Cards

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler

From: Jeanette Knox Chandler, To: Elijah H. Chandler Mailed From: Sidney, Iowa on November 11, 1886 Sidney Ia. Nov. 11th ’86 Mr. E.H. Chandler Dear Lige, Your letter dated 9th received today. I wrote you this morning and took my letter up town to mail it and where the PM handed me my mail. There was a letter from you and I was most truly glad to get it. Was sorry to hear that you was feeling badly but hope you are all right ere this. How about that bridge falling down. Was it the bridge across the river that you had reference too. If so was there anyone hurt. Oh dear! I do think that Bill Shea is the meanest man that ever stepped. Mattie ought to be very proud of her nice husband. I hope Shea and Sink will both get that they deserve and I hope this will bust them as high as a kite. I think that is a nice report and get out about Bull Shea playing poker in our house for money. I know it is not time but it is time he with some of the other boarders played cards but only for fun. I do not know anything about Pa selling whisky on Sundays. I cannot say that it is not so nor I will not say that it is so but I do hope and trust that he has not stooped so low as that. If he has, it was for that Old Schestein but I would hate to think of it being time. I do wish some one would just beat Shae into a jelly as he deserves when he does as he did to Martin Whalin. Annie and children has retired good while ago but I thought I would stay up and write you a little in answer to this letter. Of course I have not much to write now as I wrote all I could think of this morning but I thought I would write a line or two to let you know I received your letter and I knew if I went off tomorrow that I could not write then so I would stay up a while tonight. I have written a long letter to Maggie tonight also. Oh Lige I would like so well to see you but wouldn’t I talk. Why I would talk so much my tongue would be sure but Say- you don’t want to be writing me the way you have been tipping your hat at other girls for I am awful jealous. Did you know it? I was awful jealous of Annie and you because of the flirtation you two kept up all the time we were there. Ha! Ha! I’ll tell Annie about this tomorrow. Every time any one comes and asks me if I am getting homesick because I can answer Lucy does for me. She always says “Yes- she is homesick to see her fellow – Lige Chandler. He said he was going to keep me but I was not big enough to stay.” That is the answer she makes to any body. Well I did not think I could near fill up this sheet of paper when I commenced to write but I have did so and have no said anything much for foolishness. Write soon. Yours, Nettie ...

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed from: Pearl, Illinois on November 9, 1886 Mailing Address: Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Iowa Freemaut Co. Pearl, Illinois November 9th, ’86 Dear Nettie, I just received your kind letter of 6th. Just was glad to hear from you and to know you are well. I have not been well today but I feel better this eve than I did this fair morn. Haven’t had much work to do today as there is only a few trains running on account of a bridge falling down last night. We are having great times now. Everybody is at court but a few lucky ones. S. O’Shea was taken today. I think this trip will about do them up as there are several indictments on all of them. I understand Shea has made a complaint against your Pa for selling whiskey on Sundays. He says also that he had played for many at Mr. Knox’s. He thinks your Pa is the cause of him and Turk being pulled as Mr. Knox is on the grand jury. But he can’t do anything with your Pa. It’s all spite work. I just told you so you would know more about what kind of a man Shea was. They gave Martin Whalun a summons to serve on George Ottwell last night when he went in to Surk’s saloon where Ottwell was. Shea knocked him down and let Ottwell get away. I don’t know what they will do with Shea if he keeps on this way. They got Ottwell today. Everybody thinks this trip will about break Surk and Shea up. I would have old you Nettie about Johnny but I didn’t want you to be grieving about it when I knew it could do not good. I am sorry that its so. I think the meeting will close tonight. I think they are getting discouraged. Lizzie F. is up at Mr. Hess’ again. Said Hess and Mrs. Hess was down to Pearl yesterday but Lizzie stayed to keep house while they were gone. Johnny K and Charley Ottwell went over the river last night to keep out of the way of the Sheriff. My hat just lands up in grand style with new work in it. Sunday every time I went where Maggie and Lizzie was I tip my hat at them. Lizzie said I thought I was awful smart. Very truly yours, EHC ...

From: William Knox, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: William Knox, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Villisca, IA on November 7th, 1886 Dear Sister Nettie, Yours received. Did think when I wrote that I would come to Sidney today, but give the trip up as I think that Johnnie will come out to Ima soon and we will come down then. I would like to come down so as to congratulate among the boys over the major election but the expense is to much when a person thinks of it. I am now taking my regulars at this hotel. Have been here about two weeks, like the place very well with the exception of the room. It is too small for any use. Have not heard from home since I wrote you. Am expecting a letter soon. Write me at your convenience soon and give me all the Sidney news. Regards to all inquiring friends. Your Bro, Will ...

November 7, 1886 · Post Cards

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: St. Lovack, R.P.O on November3, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freenaut Co Peace Holy November 3rd ’86 Dear Nettie, I will not try and answer your kind letter that I received today. I had been looking for a letter two or three days. I should have written to you yesterday only I knew I had 2 or 3 letters that were not answered yet and thought I would wait one day more. The Methodist are holding a protected meeting now. I have been going all the time ’til tonight. Thought I would rather stay at home and write to you than to go to Church as I find more pleasure writing to you than anything else while I cannot be with you. I don’t want you to get mad about the short letter I wrote you today. I did not have time to write any. I wanted to get a letter started as soon as possible so I could hear from you again that much sooner. A week seems like a month to me when I don’t hear from you. I took a message this evening from Sidney to your Pa, when the Just Commenced Spelling Sydney. I was awful anxious to hear what it was going to be there. Was a thousand thoughts in my head. I was afraid somebody was sick. I was glad when I heard that it was about the Election. Your Pa is in Pittsfield on the grand jury. I forwarded the telegram to him at Pittsfield there. I bought a copy for our house and gave it to Maggy. She flew around the table to get it. When I told her it was from Sidney she said she would bet you was coming. Had then the thought it was from Willie. No I did not know Lizzie H. had to set up while Ian was there. They have not got any stove up yet. Ian was down last Sunday about knowing whether he stayed all night or not. Did you hear about Frageus Boby dying. It died last Friday, I think. very body has have election on the Bra for the last week. I am glad it is over now. The Eutrise Democratic ticket was elected in the county. Yes Nettie I wish I could have been there last Sunday night. I can’t think of any thing that would be more enjoyment for me than to be with you. I think of you every time I come to the house and many many other times. I think more of you every time I hear from you. Seems to me like because I think you mean what you say. Yes, I know you do. I don’t think you could have any object in being false. Nettie I will tell you again that I never had half as much confidence in any one as I have in you and while I have I assure you that I will never give you any room to complain. I will be awful glad when I get the hat work you are making. Wish you would come with it and fit in my hat. Do you remember the heart you sewed in my hat once. There is a big difference now, you know time. Some time changes mind but not in all cases. I know nothing will ever change my mind in regard to you. Do you remember about George Aker’s wife dying just a short time before you left. I understand he is going to get married tomorrow to May Miller. I think he is in a hurry, don’t you? I am getting cold now. I will close for tonight. Hope will hear from soon. Yours Truly, E.H. Chandler I wish you was here Nettie ...

November 3, 1886 · Post Cards

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler

From: Elijah H. Chandler, To: Jeanette Knox Chandler Mailed From: Pearl, Illinois on November 3, 1886 Miss Nettie Knox Sidney, Ia Freemont Co Pearl Ill 3rd ’86 Dear Nettie, Your very kind letter just received but not before I was looking for it. I think it has been a week today since I got a letter from you. I will try to write you a few lines today so can send it off on this eve train. I have so awful much to do today I can not write much but will finish tonight. I don’t know how I would pass the time away if was not for the hopes I have in the future. When you are lonesome and thinking of home you can always remember that I think of you every day and want you to come home as bad as you want to come. My work for the month has to be done up now. Yesterday was Election day and the boys wanted the return by wire and I agreed to get them from 35 states for six dollars. So I have not had time to do anything today only copy bulletins of the election. Oh yes I will be pleased when I get the hat works. I must close now but will write you a letter tonight and give you all the news. Then you will receive it the next day after you get this. Give Anna my regards. I remain yours. Truly, EHC ...

From: Elijah H., Dave, and J. Saul Chandler, To: Unknown Chandler

From: Elijah H., Dave, and J. Saul Chandler, To: Unknown Chandler Mailed On: October 24, 1886 October the 24, 1886 Dear Brother, Yours of the 26 is received. Was glad to hear from you and hear you and all was well. I was sorry to hear of the Death of Mactard Bell Allen but we have all got to Dye some time. That we may be fine forever here after. I truly hope you will have a good time when you get married. I hope you will not have any brake up before the day comes. Well I have some news to write of any importance. Today is Sunday I just got back from hunting. I got 2 turkeys. Will have a good dinner tomorrow. There is 30 miles of the R.R. done. 40 more will come to precut. Not much mining going on at present. Everybody in good spirit. I think we will have a break in the spring. Will close hopping to hear from you soon. Goodbye, J. Saul Chandler E.H. Chandler Dave Chandler ...
